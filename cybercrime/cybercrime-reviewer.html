What is a computer?

A machine that performs processes and calculations based on instructions from software or hardware programs. Two categories of parts: hardware (physical structure) and software (operating system and applications). By size: supercomputer (fastest, largest, most expensive), mainframe (a bit smaller), personal computer (everyday use).

Hardware

Input devices

Enter data: keyboard, mouse, scanner, microphone, digital camera, PC video camera.

Output devices

Give information to the user: monitor (screen), printer (hard copy), speaker (sound).

Software and storage

System software (OS)
Runs the computer and controls its devices (Windows, macOS). Application software cannot work without it.
Application software
Lets users do specific tasks: word processing (Word), spreadsheet (Excel), e-mail (Outlook), internet browser.
Storage media
Hard drive (internal), floppy disk (portable, small), CD and DVD (more capacity, some rewritable), USB flash drive (tiny, high capacity).

Computer care

Keep dust and food away, use clean hands, treat it with respect, avoid use during storms, use antivirus and get permission before downloading files, and handle CDs carefully. Scanning, defragging, and reformatting are left to experts.

History of the internet

1962 ARPA research→ARPANET→1 Jan 1983 TCP/IP→1990 World Wide Web (Tim Berners-Lee)

ARPA (US Department of Defense) laid the groundwork for ARPANET. Universities joined, growth exploded, and hypertext-based WWW made navigation easy.

Meaning of the internet

A global system of interconnected computer networks using the TCP/IP protocol suite. It lets connected computers exchange text, graphics, voice, video, and programs. Key uses: browsing, e-mail, streaming, online commerce.

Cybercrime: concepts and typologies

Computer as target, tool, or evidence.

What is cybercrime?

A crime involving a computer and a network. The computer may be used to commit the crime, or may be its target. It includes offenses against individuals or groups with a criminal motive to harm reputation or cause physical, mental, or financial harm through the internet (chat rooms, e-mail, boards) and mobile phones (Bluetooth, SMS, MMS). Computer crime has two elements: computer and crime. Charles Babbage, with his analytical engine, is called a “father of the computer.”

Computer as target

Needs technical knowledge: hacking, viruses and worms, denial of service, malware.

Computer as tool / weapon

Less technical, exploits human weakness: internet fraud, spamming, phishing, carding, cyber terrorism, pornography. Damage is often psychological.

Cyber security and safety tips

Protects systems from unauthorized access, damage, or being made inaccessible. Tips: use antivirus software, install firewalls, uninstall unnecessary software, maintain backups, check security settings.

Four offense types (Convention on Cybercrime)

  1. Offenses against confidentiality, integrity and availability of computer data and systems (illegal access, interception, data and system interference, misuse of devices)
  2. Computer-related offenses (forgery, fraud)
  3. Content-related offenses (child pornography)
  4. Copyright-related offenses (infringement)

Classification of computer crimes

Financial and internet fraud
Intentional deception for gain. Internet fraud tricks victims out of money via chat rooms, e-mail, boards, websites.
Computer fraud
Dishonest misrepresentation to cause loss: altering input data, altering or suppressing output, altering or deleting stored data.
Bank fraud
Obtaining a financial institution’s money or assets by illegal means; white-collar crime.
Carding
Using a stolen credit card to charge pre-paid or gift cards.
Identity theft
Obtaining personally identifiable information to impersonate someone.
Extortion
Getting money, property, or services through coercion. Cyber-extortion: threatening repeated DoS attacks unless paid.
Cyberterrorism
Premeditated, politically motivated attack on information systems that threatens or causes violence.
Cyberwarfare
Attacks against a nation-state (e.g., Estonia 2007, Georgia 2008).

Malware, spam, and phishing

  • Malware: any intentionally harmful program: viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware, spyware.
  • Spamming: unwanted bulk messages sent indiscriminately (e-mail, forums, texts, social media).
  • Phishing: disguising as a trustworthy entity to obtain usernames, passwords, card details, usually via e-mail spoofing to a fake lookalike site. A social engineering technique.

Harassment, stalking, and other content

  • Obscene or offensive content: legality varies by country; child pornography is illegal in most jurisdictions.
  • Online harassment: targets specific people (gender, race, religion, etc.); includes revenge porn. “True threats” are not protected speech.
  • Drug trafficking: darknet markets (e.g., Silk Road) and encrypted messaging.

Types of stalking

Surveillance (physical following), cyber (electronic means), aggravated (restraint, bodily harm, or violating a protection order). Stalking needs two or more occasions of proximity, non-consensual communication, or threats.

Types of stalker

Rejected, resentful (revenge), predatory (power, sexual gratification), intimacy seeker, incompetent suitor, erotomania / morbidly infatuated.

Investigation and the hacker

A computer can be a source of evidence (digital forensics); log files may hold records of value. Investigations often begin with an IP address trace, though it is not always conclusive. Hacking knowledge now spreads widely online, so technical skill is less of a barrier. A hacker is someone proficient at programming and finding vulnerabilities; the term has taken on a negative meaning in crime.

RA 10175: Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012

An act defining cybercrime and providing for prevention, investigation, suppression, and penalties.

Background and history

  • Preceded by the Electronic Commerce Act of 2000 (RA 8792).
  • Need: the ILOVEYOU virus (Onel de Guzman) exposed that existing laws gave no legal basis to criminalize computer crimes.
  • Born from HB 5808 (Rep. Susan Yap-Sulit and 36 co-authors) and Senate Bill 2976 (Sen. Edgardo Angara); passed June 5 and 4, 2012; signed by President Benigno Aquino III on September 12, 2012.
  • The libel clause: Sen. Sotto introduced the amendment on January 24, 2012, saying internet libel would make people more cautious.

Key terms

Access
Instructing, communicating with, storing or retrieving data in, or otherwise using a computer system’s resources.
Cyber
A computer or computer network; the electronic medium of online communication.
Interception
Listening to, recording, monitoring, or surveilling the content of communications, directly or through eavesdropping devices.

OffenseMeaning
Illegal accessIntentional access to a computer system, in whole or part, without right
Illegal interceptionIntentional interception by technical means (exception: service provider employees for quality checks, not random monitoring)
Data interferenceIntentional or reckless alteration, damage, deletion, or deterioration of computer data
System interferenceIntentional alteration or reckless hindering of a computer or network’s functioning
Misuse of devicesUse, production, sale, procurement, importation, distribution of devices or passwords to commit offenses; possession with intent to use
Cyber-squattingAcquiring a domain name in bad faith to profit, mislead, or deprive others, when it is identical or confusingly similar to a trademark or a person’s name

Sec. 4(b): computer-related offenses

Computer-related forgery
Inputting, altering, or deleting data without right to produce unauthentic data, intending it be treated as authentic; or knowingly using such data.
Computer-related fraud
Unauthorized input, alteration, or deletion of data or programs, or interference with a system, including phishing, to gain economic benefit.
Computer-related identity theft
Intentional acquisition, use, misuse, transfer, possession, alteration, or deletion of identifying information of another without right.

Sec. 4(c): content-related offenses

Cybersex
Interactive prostitution or obscenity through cyberspace using webcams.
Child pornography
Acts under RA 9775 committed via computer; penalty one degree higher.
Unsolicited commercial communications
Prohibited unless prior consent, or with a simple opt-out, no disguised source, and no misleading information.
Libel
Art. 355 of the Revised Penal Code, committed through a computer system.

Secs. 5 to 9: other offenses and liability

  • Sec. 5: aiding or abetting, and attempt to commit cybercrime, are punishable.
  • Sec. 6: crimes under the Revised Penal Code and special laws committed through ICT: penalty one degree higher.
  • Sec. 7: prosecution is without prejudice to liability under the RPC or special laws.
  • Sec. 9 (corporate liability): based on power of representation, authority to decide, or authority to exercise control. Fine at least double; officers directly responsible are jointly and severally liable; foreign corporations: persons directly responsible.

Enforcement and procedure

  • Law enforcement: PNP and NBI (Sec. 10).
  • Real-time traffic data collection (Sec. 12): with due cause. Traffic data covers origin, destination, route, time, date, size, duration, type of service, not content or identities.
  • Court warrant requires written application, examination under oath, reasonable grounds that a crime was, is being, or is about to be committed; that evidence is essential; and that no other means are readily available.

Jurisdiction and agencies

  • Jurisdiction: Regional Trial Court, if committed in the Philippines or using a computer system wholly or partly in the country.
  • DOJ: central authority; extends assistance; issues orders to restrict or block access to data prima facie in violation (Sec. 19).
  • CICC (Sec. 18): Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center, an inter-agency body under the Office of the President, created within 30 days of effectivity.
  • Sec. 29: repealing clause; modifies Sec. 33(a) of RA 8792.
