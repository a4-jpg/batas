Definition of Key Terminologies

Fire

The manifestation of rapid chemical reaction occurring between fuel and an oxidizer (typically oxygen in air). Such reaction releases energy in the form of heat and light. The active principle of burning, characterized by heat and light of combustion.

Pyrolysis

Chemical decomposition of matter through the action of heat. Causes a change from a solid state to vapour state. If the vapour mixes sufficiently with air and is heated to high temperature, combustion results.

Combustion

A self-sustaining chemical reaction producing energy or products that cause more reactions of the same kind. Commonly known as burning.

Spontaneous Combustion

The process of catching fire as a result of heat generated by internal chemical action — auto-ignition due to the gradual rising of heat in a confined space until ignition temperature is reached.

Flame

Incandescent gases which accompany brisk or rapid oxidation of any combustible material. The burning gas or vapor of a fire that is visible as light of various colors.

Products of Combustion

The 4 Products of Combustion

ProductDescription
Fire GasesThose that remain after other products have cooled to normal temperature.
FlameThe luminous body of a burning gas. Becomes hotter and less luminous when mixed with more oxygen.
HeatA form of energy measured in degrees of temperature. The product of combustion that spreads fire.
SmokeVisible product of incomplete combustion. Usually a mixture of oxygen, nitrogen, CO₂, CO, and finely divided particles of soot and carbon.

Fire Triangle vs. Fire Tetrahedron

Fire Triangle

Three Elements (Glowing Mode)

  • Heat — the trigger/source of ignition (open flame, electrical sparks, friction, hot surfaces, lightning)
  • Fuel — the material that burns (solid, liquid, or gas): wood, gasoline, LPG, plastics, clothing
  • Oxygen — supports combustion; comes from surrounding air (~21% of air by volume)

🔺 Removing ANY one element extinguishes the fire.

Fire Tetrahedron

Four Elements (Flaming Mode)

  • Oxygen (Oxidizing Agent) — supports the combustion process
  • Fuel (Reducing Agent) — material or substance being burned
  • Heat (Temperature) — raises fuel to its ignition temperature
  • Self-Sustained Chemical Chain Reaction — 4th element; a series of continuous reactions where products of one initiate and sustain the next

🔷 Explains the flaming mode of combustion. 16% O₂ = minimum for heat; 21% = normal.

Characteristics of Fire

The 4 Characteristics of Fire

FAST
Fast

Takes only minutes for thick black smoke and flames to fill a house.

HOT
Hot

Room temp can be 100°F at floor level and rise to 600°F near the ceiling.

DARK
Dark

Starts bright but quickly produces black smoke and complete darkness.

DEADLY
Deadly

Smoke and toxic gases kill more people than flames do.

Fuel (Reducing Agent)

Fuel

Anything that will burn when heated with sufficient oxygen. The most important element of the fire triangle — it is what burns. Comes in three forms: solid, liquid, or gas. SOLID = molecules closely packed. LIQUID = loosely packed. GAS = free to move.

TypeDescription / Examples
Pyrolyzable Solid FuelsOrdinary combustibles: wood, paper, etc. — undergo pyrolysis to become flammable vapors.
Non-Pyrolyzable Solid FuelsDifficult to ignite; no pyrolyzable elements (e.g., charcoal).
BiomassReplaceable organic matter (wood, garbage, animal manure) used to produce energy.
Fabrics & TextilesFiber-based combustibles.
PlasticsCellulose Nitrate (pyroxylin) — a chemical powder also used in bombs.
CoalBlack, combustible mineral solid from partial decomposition of matter.
PeatPartially decayed plant matter found in swamps.

Mainly made from Petroleum (crude oil) refined into gasoline, diesel oil, and kerosene.

TypeFlash Point
Flammable LiquidsFlash point below 37.8°C (100°F) and vapor pressure not exceeding 40 psia.
Combustible LiquidsFlash point at or above 37.8°C (100°F).

Gaseous fuels: molecules in rapid movement and random motion; no definite shape or volume. Examples: acetylene, propane, butane.

ClassificationTypes
By SourceNatural Gas — chiefly methane, mixed with foul-smelling sulfur compounds to detect leaks; Butane and propane become LPG/LNG under pressure.
Manufactured Gas — produced from coal, petroleum, or biomass through heating and chemical procedures.
By Physical CharacteristicCompressed Gas — exists solely in gaseous state under pressure.
Liquefied Gas — partly liquid, partly gaseous inside container (e.g., LPG).
Cryogenic Gas — liquefied at temperatures far below normal atmospheric temperature.
By UsageFuel Gases — used for burning with air (natural gas, LPG).
Industrial Gases — welding (O₂, acetylene), refrigeration (freon), etc.
Medical Gases — anesthesia (nitrous oxide), respiratory therapy (O₂).
Other Fuels

Chemical & Nuclear Fuels

  • CHEMICALProduced in solid and liquid form; create great amounts of heat and power; used chiefly in rocket engines. Common rocket fuel: hydrazine.
  • NUCLEARProvide energy through fission (splitting nucleus) or fusion (combining two light nuclei) of atoms.
04

Heat, Temperature & Energy

Heat

The energy component of the fire tetrahedron. When in contact with a fuel, it supports the combustion reaction. The source of ignition — can be a safety match, lighted candle, or more sophisticated chemical, mechanical, or electrical contrivance designed to start a blaze.

Temperature

Measurement of the degree of thermal agitation of molecules; the hotness or coldness of something. Measured by a thermometer; expressed in °C, °F, and °K.

ScaleKey Points
CelsiusFreezing: 0°C | Boiling: 100°C. Widely used for scientific works.
FahrenheitFreezing: 32°F | Boiling: 212°F. Used in English-speaking countries for non-scientific purposes.
Kelvin (Absolute)Most common thermodynamic scale. Absolute zero = –273.15°C / –459.67°F.
RankineAbsolute zero base; 1°R = 1°F. Freezing: 492°R | Boiling: 672°R.
International Temperature Scale (1933)Adopted by 31 nations; based on Kelvin scale; uses platinum wire as standard (–190° to 660°C).
Sources of Heat — Types of Energy

Common Sources of Heat Energy

  • CHEMICALMost common source in combustion reactions. Heat generated when any combustible contacts oxygen (oxidation). Example: burning match, spontaneous heating.
  • ELECTRICALCan generate temperatures high enough to ignite combustibles. Includes: over current/overload, arcing, sparking, static lightning.
  • NUCLEARGenerated when atoms split (fission) or combine (fusion). Example: nuclear reactors, solar energy (fusion).
  • MECHANICALCreated by friction (two surfaces moving against each other) and compression (heat generated when gas is compressed).
Heat Transfer

Three Methods of Heat Transfer

CONDUCTION
Conduction

Transfer of heat by molecular activity within a material/medium (usually a solid). Direct contact is the underlying factor.

CONVECTION
Convection

Transfer of heat through a circulating medium (usually air or liquid). Chiefly responsible for the spread of fire in structures.

RADIATION
Radiation

Heat moves in waves and rays like sunlight at 186,000 miles/second. Energy travels through materials and space as waves.

Latent Heat

Latent Heat & Phase Changes

The quantity of heat absorbed by a substance when it changes phase. Specific Heat = heat required to raise 1 unit mass of a substance by 1 degree.

Phase ChangeProcess
SublimationSolid → Gas
MeltingSolid → Liquid
VaporizationLiquid → Vapor/Gas
CondensationSteam/Vapor/Gas → Water

Colorless, odorless gas. Air composition: 21% oxygen, 78% nitrogen, 1% other gases.

O₂ LevelEffect

Fire Behavior, Stages & Dangerous Conditions

Thermal Balance

The rising movement or normal pattern of fire — normal behavior when the pattern is undisturbed.

Thermal Imbalance

The abnormal movement of fire due to the interference of foreign matter. Often confuses the fire investigator in determining the exact point of origin.

Dangerous Fire Conditions

Fatal Fire Behaviors

The sudden and rapid (violent) burning of heated gases in a confined area that occurs in the form of an explosion. Occurs because of improper ventilation. When a door or window is suddenly opened in an improperly ventilated room, the room violently sucks oxygen from outside, causing a sudden combustion explosion.

The sudden ignition of accumulated radical gases produced during incomplete combustion of fuels. Initiated by a spark or flash when temperature reaches flash point. Accumulated radical gases suddenly burn, creating very intense fire capable of causing flames to jump (fireball) that can travel to 100 yards in seconds.

A fatal condition where the fire resists extinguishment operations and becomes stronger and bigger instead.

Better known as dust explosion. Happens when a metal post completely covered with dust is hit by lightning. Dust particles burn simultaneously, creating a violent chemical reaction with a very bright flash followed by an explosion.

Three Stages of Fire

Incipient → Free Burning → Smoldering

StageCharacteristics
1. Incipient Phase (Initial Stage) Normal room temperature. Base temperature: 400–800/1,000°F. Ceiling temp: ~200°F. Pyrolysis products are mostly water vapor and CO₂; small quantities of CO and sulfides may be present.
2. Free Burning Phase Materials burning in presence of adequate oxygen. Accelerated pyrolysis. Development of convection currents. Base temp: 800–1,000/1,330°F. Ceiling: 1,200–1,600°F. Pyrolytic decomposition moves upward (fire fingerprints). Occurrence of flashover.
3. Smoldering Phase Flame ceases; dense smoke and heat fill the room. Oxygen drops to 13% or below. Products of incomplete combustion increase (CO, ignition temp ~1,125°F). Ceiling temp: 1,000–1,300°F. Heat and pressure build up. When sufficient oxygen is introduced → BACKDRAFT.
5 Stages of Fire

Ignition → Growth → Flashover → Fully Developed → Decay

  • 1Ignition — the four elements of fire tetrahedron come together; combustion begins.
  • 2Growth — a fire plume forms above burning fuel; draws/entrains air from surrounding space.
  • 3Flashover — transition between growth and fully developed stages; all exposed combustible surfaces ignite.
  • 4Fully Developed — all combustible materials in the compartment are involved in the fire.
  • 5Decay — fire consumes available fuel; rate of heat released begins to decline.

Causes & Classifications of Fire

By Cause

Four Classifications by Cause

Fires without direct human intervention.

  • Spontaneous Heating — automatic chemical reaction resulting in spontaneous combustion; gradual rising of heat in a confined space; sun rays focused through glasses acting as a convex lens.
  • Lightning — a form of static electricity; a natural current with great magnitude.
    • Hot Bolt — longer duration; can only ignite combustibles; may be gradual or rapid.
    • Cold Bolt — shorter duration; capable of splintering property or blowing apart an entire structure; produces tremendous amperage and very high temperature.

Purely accidental causes, negligence, and other forms of human error. Electrical accidents include:

  • Short Circuit — unusual/accidental connections between two points at different potentials in an electrical circuit of relatively low resistance.
  • Arcing — production of sustained luminous electrical discharge between separated electrodes; electrical current crosses a gap between two conductors.
  • Sparking — production of incandescent particles when two different potentials come in contact; occurs during short circuits or welding operations.
  • Induced Current — sudden increase of electrical current resulting in burning of insulating materials, explosion of fuse box, or burning of electrical appliances.
  • Overheating of Appliances — increase in amperage while current flows, damaging insulating materials (internal or external).

Deliberately set under circumstances where the person knows the fire should not be set. Indicators include preparations or traces of accelerant, plants, and trailers.

TermDefinition
AccelerantHighly flammable chemicals used to facilitate flame propagation.
PlantThe preparation and/or gathering of combustible materials needed to start a fire.
TrailerThe preparation of flammable substances in order to spread the fire.

Whenever the cause cannot be proven, the proper classification is undetermined.

Laws & Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP)

Republic Act No. 6975 — the DILG Act of 1990 (Chapter 4, Sec. 53–59) created the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP).

  • Responsible for prevention and suppression of all destructive fires.
  • BFP enforces the Fire Code and other related laws.
  • Has power to investigate all causes of fires and file proper complaints with the city or provincial prosecutor.

Key Distinctions:

TermMeaning
Fire SuppressionSlowing down the rate of burning.
Fire ControlKeeping the fire from spreading / holding fire to one area.
Fire ExtinguishmentPutting the fire completely out.

Provides for the professionalization of the BFP and BJMP. Amended certain provisions of RA 6975.

Key Appointments (Sec. 5, RA 9263):

  • Fire/Jail Officer I to Senior Fire/Jail Officer IV — Appointed by Regional Director; attested by CSC.
  • Fire/Jail Inspector to Superintendent — Appointed by Chief of the Fire Bureau; attested by CSC.
  • Senior Superintendent — Appointed by Secretary of the DILG upon recommendation of Chief; attested by CSC.
  • Chief Superintendent to Director — Appointed by the President upon recommendation of Secretary of the DILG; endorsed by Chairman of the CSC.

⚠️ Chief of Fire Bureau serves a tour of duty not exceeding 4 years. Cannot be someone retirable within 6 months.

Fire Fighting Operations

Operations Sequence

Fire Fighting Operations Procedures (In Order)

  • 1Pre-Fire Planning — developing systematic course of actions to realize the objectives of fire protection; establishing SOP in case fire breaks out.
  • 2Evaluation – Size-Up — on-the-spot planning; mental evaluation by the OIC to determine the appropriate course of action. Learn facts (5Ws 1H), understand fire behavior probabilities, know own capabilities, determine specific course of action.
  • 3Evacuation — transferring people, livestock, and property away from the burning area to minimize damage.
  • 4Entry — accessing the burning structure. Forcible entry purposes: access for firefighters, rescue, aid in ventilation.
  • 5Rescue — removing/extricating people and livestock from the burning building; conveying them to a secure place.
  • 6Exposure — securing buildings near the burning structure to prevent fire from extending.
  • 7Confinement — restricting fire at the place it started; preventing extension from one section to another.
  • 8Ventilation — displacing toxic gases and heated atmosphere within the building with normal air. Types: (1) Vertical — top to bottom; (2) Cross/Horizontal — opens windows; (3) Mechanical Force — uses smoke ejector.
  • 9Salvage — protecting properties from preventable damage. Steps: remove material outside, cover/protect materials with tarpaulins.
  • 10Extinguishment — putting out the main body of fire using 4 general methods.
  • 11Overhaul — complete and detailed check of the structure to eliminate conditions that may cause re-flash; extinguishment of sparks or smoldering substances (embers) to prevent re-ignition.
  • 12Fire Scene Investigation — final stage; inquiry to determine the origin and cause of fire.
Four Methods of Extinguishment

General Methods of Fire Extinguishment

METHOD 1
Temperature Reduction

Cooling the temperature, usually done with water. Lowers temperature until fuel does not produce sufficient vapors.

METHOD 2
Fuel Removal

Eliminating the fuel supply: stop liquid fuel flow, prevent flammable gas production, remove solid fuel, or allow fire to burn until fuel is consumed.

METHOD 3
Oxygen Dilution

Reduction of oxygen concentration by introducing inert gases or separating oxygen from the fuel.

METHOD 4
Chemical Inhibition

Agents like dry chemical and halon interrupt flame production. Effective only on burning gas and liquid fuels (not smoldering mode).

Fire Prevention Methods

Three Main Methods of Fire Prevention

  • 1Laws and Regulations — codes and standards requiring fire retardant materials and proper electrical wiring in buildings.
  • 2Inspection of Buildings — fire brigades and public agencies inspect buildings for fire hazards and recommend corrective action.
  • 3Public Education — education about fire safety and prevention; teaching children and adults about fire hazards throughout the community.

Fire Extinguisher

A mechanical device (usually metal) containing chemicals, fluids, or gases for stopping fires. A portable device used to put out fires of limited size before they propagate.

PASS Method

Operating Procedure — P.A.S.S.

  • PPull the pin at the top of the extinguisher that keeps the handle from being pressed.
  • AAim the nozzle or outlet towards the fire — point at the BASE of the fire.
  • SSqueeze the handle above the carrying handle to discharge the extinguishing agent.
  • SSweep the nozzle sideways at the BASE of the flame to disperse the extinguishing agent.

Fire Detection Systems & Ladders

Fire Detectors

Classification of Fire Detectors

Designed to react and activate alarm when products of combustion enter their sensors. Attached to ceiling or wall.

TypeHow It Works
Ionization DetectorUses a radioactive source to transform air inside into a conductor of electrical current. Visible or invisible products of combustion entering the chamber interrupt current flow → activates alarm.
Photoelectric Smoke DetectorUses a projected light beam (large areas) or a reflected light beam/spot type detector (small areas).
TypeHow It Works
Rate-of-Rise DetectorCalibrated so a rapid increase in room temperature activates the alarm. Allows temperature within a certain range but automatically activates when the permitted maximum is surpassed.
Fixed-Temperature DetectorPreset to activate at a given temperature. May come with a calibration screw to reset to avoid false alarms.

A network of pipes installed throughout a building carrying water to nozzles in the ceiling. The heat from a fire causes the nozzles directly above the fire to open and spray water.

Two main categories: Ground ladders (10–55 ft.) and Aerial ladders.

Purposes: rescue, stretch line into fire building, provide ventilation access.

TypeDescription
Straight / Hook LadderOne section only; ranges from 12–16 ft. (common: 14 ft.).
Extension LadderConsists of a bed and one or more fly ladders. With stay pole = tormentors. Up to 35 ft.; over 35 ft. = Bangor Ladder.
Attic LadderProvides access to difficult places; can be folded or collapsed.
Wall LadderBest for rescue where ladder falls short; allows climbing/descending one storey at a time.
Aerial LadderMounted on a turntable; extends up to 100 ft.; 3 or 4 metal fly sections; controlled by hydraulically controlled cables.

⚡ Key Takeaways for the Board Exam

  • Fire = rapid chemical reaction between fuel and oxidizer releasing heat and light.
  • Fire Triangle (3 elements: Heat + Fuel + Oxygen) → Glowing mode. Fire Tetrahedron (4 elements: + Chemical Chain Reaction) → Flaming mode.
  • Products of combustion: Fire Gases, Flame, Heat, Smoke. Fire characteristics: Fast, Hot, Dark, Deadly.
  • Flammable liquids: flash point BELOW 37.8°C. Combustible liquids: AT OR ABOVE 37.8°C.
  • 21% O₂ = normal fire; 12% = insufficient; 13% or below = flame ceases (Smoldering Phase).
  • Backdraft = sudden burning in confined area (improper ventilation, explosion). Flashover = sudden ignition of accumulated radical gases. Biteback = fire resists extinguishment.
  • Three Stages: Incipient → Free Burning → Smoldering. Five Stages: Ignition → Growth → Flashover → Fully Developed → Decay.
  • Classes: A (ordinary), B (liquid), C (electrical), D (metallic).
  • Causes: Natural/Providential, Accidental, Intentional (Incendiary), Undetermined.
  • Accelerant = flammable chemical to facilitate flame propagation. Plant = gathering combustibles. Trailer = spreading substances.
  • RA 6975 created the BFP. RA 9263 = BFP Professionalization Act of 2004. Chief BFP = appointed by President; max 4-year tour of duty.
  • Municipal (≤₱20M) → City (≤₱30M) → District/Provincial (₱30–40M) → Regional (₱40–60M) → National BFP (above ₱60M).
  • PASS: Pull → Aim → Squeeze → Sweep. Dry Chemical extinguishers = Class A, B, C. Dry Powder = Class D.
  • Ionization detector = radioactive source. Photoelectric = light beam. Rate-of-rise = rapid temp increase. Fixed-temperature = preset temp.
  • 12 firefighting operation steps: Pre-Planning → Size-Up → Evacuation → Entry → Rescue → Exposure → Confinement → Ventilation → Salvage → Extinguishment → Overhaul → Fire Scene Investigation.
