What is I/O Psychology?

Definition, scope, and the two-sided coin of the field.

Core definition

DefinitionIndustrial and organizational psychology is the study of people working and the application of the principles of psychology to the organizational and work environment.

It is a field concerned with both the study of psychological principles as a science and the application of those principles in real workplaces (Spector, 2008; Jex, 2002). The central goals are improving productivity and creating the best possible working environment for employees.

Apart from sleeping, the average person spends more hours per week working than any other activity. That much time given to an activity deeply impacts a person's life — in both positive and negative ways. Studying how the working world affects behavior and psychological makeup is therefore a major focus of psychology.

The two-sided coin

🏭 The Industrial Side

A top-down perspective. Focuses on understanding human behavior to improve:

  • Organizational efficiency
  • Employee selection
  • Employee training
  • Job design

Views behavior to assess ways in which the organization can benefit from psychological principles.

🤝 The Organizational Side

A bottom-up perspective. Focuses on understanding behavior to enhance:

  • Employee satisfaction
  • Well-being in the workplace
  • Employee attitudes & behavior
  • Job stress & supervisory practices

Improves the quality of life of individuals within the organization rather than the organization as a whole.

Together — not mutually exclusive

"Motivation is relevant to the [industrial] concerns of employee efficiency and performance, but it is also relevant to the [organizational] concern with the happiness and well-being of employees." — Spector (2008)

Though each side approaches from a different perspective, they are not mutually exclusive in their goals, applications, or topics of interest. The same concept — like motivation — can be studied from both lenses simultaneously.

Historical Overview

From the early 20th century to the modern field — origins, key figures, and pivotal events.

Early pioneers (pre-WWI)

I/O psychology had its origins in the early 20th century. Several influential psychologists studied issues we would now categorize as industrial psychology. Notably, Cattell, Münsterberg, and Scott had all been students of Wilhelm Wundt, the father of experimental psychology.

James Cattell (1860–1944)
Columbia. Founded the Psychological Corporation, still operating today. Influenced students in industrial psychology.
Hugo Münsterberg (1863–1916)
Harvard. Published Psychology and Industrial Efficiency (1913), covering employee selection, training, and effective advertising.
Walter Dill Scott (1869–1955)
Northwestern. First psychologist to apply psychology to advertising, management, and personnel selection. Published The Theory of Advertising and Psychology of Advertising (1903).
Robert Yerkes (1876–1956)
Dartmouth. President of the APA at the start of WWI; organized psychological screening for the military.
Walter Bingham (1880–1952)
Dartmouth. Collaborated with Yerkes; later became chief psychologist for the War Department in WWII.
Lillian Gilbreth (1878–1972)
Purdue. Known as the mother of modern management. Pioneer of time-and-motion studies; first woman in the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (1926).

World War I — a catalyst

The United States entered WWI in April 1917, galvanizing psychologists to contribute to the military effort.

Army Alpha Test

Developed under the Surgeon General's Office (SGO) led by Yerkes. Measured mental abilities for literate English-speaking draftees.

Army Beta Test

A non-verbal form of the test administered to illiterate and non-English-speaking draftees. Ensured broader screening coverage.

Scott and Bingham, under the Adjutant General's Office (AGO), created a catalogue of occupational needs for the Army — essentially a job-description system and a system of performance ratings for officers.

The Hawthorne Studies (1929–1932)

From 1929–1932, Elton Mayo (1880–1949) and colleagues conducted a landmark series of studies at Western Electric's Hawthorne Works near Chicago.

Key FindingThe Hawthorne Effect — the increase in performance of individuals who are noticed, watched, and paid attention to by researchers or supervisors. Any change in a variable (even negative) led to improved productivity as long as attention was present. The effect faded when attention faded.

These studies began as research into physical work environment factors (e.g., lighting), but researchers found psychological and social factors were more significant. They mark the origin of organizational psychology.

1930s — Feelings, leadership, and group dynamics

Kurt Lewin conducted influential research on leadership styles, team structure, and group dynamics. He is considered the founder of social psychology. His key contributions:

  • Coined the term group dynamics
  • Studied the effect of leadership style on aggression, group dynamics, and satisfaction (using children as subjects)
  • Research on group interactions, cooperation, competition, and communication

Researchers also began studying employees' feelings about their jobs — leading to interest in job satisfaction and employee motivation.

Frederick Taylor & Scientific Management

Frederick Taylor was an engineer who designed the workplace to maximize output and wages simultaneously. In 1911 he published The Principles of Scientific Management.

Key Experiment

Workers handling heavy iron ingots. Introducing work rests increased productivity from 12.5 → 47.0 tons/day with less fatigue and higher wages. Company cost dropped from 9.2¢ → 3.9¢ per ton.

Criticisms

Critics argued it would exploit workers and reduce headcount. The underlying assumption that only a manager could determine the most efficient work method was also controversial. Taylor believed workers were fundamentally lazy.

Lillian Gilbreth & Human Efficiency

Gilbreth used time and motion studies with her husband Frank to reduce the number of motions needed to perform tasks. She investigated employee fatigue and found many workers were motivated by money and job satisfaction.

In 1914 she published The Psychology of Management: The Function of the Mind in Determining, Teaching, and Installing Methods of Least Waste. Known as the mother of modern management.

Gilbreth's Everyday InnovationsShelves inside refrigerator doors · Foot-pedal-operated trash can lids

Awarded the Hoover Medal of the American Society of Civil Engineers in 1966. First woman to join the American Society of Mechanical Engineers in 1926.

Ergonomics ConnectionTaylor and Gilbreth's work on machine–human fit led to the study of ergonomics, also known as human factors psychology.

World War II — field expansion

WWII again drove I/O psychology's expansion. Walter Bingham was hired as chief psychologist for the War Department and developed new systems for:

  • Job selection and classification
  • Training and performance review
  • Team development and morale change
  • Attitude change methods

Other nations — Canada and the UK — saw parallel growth. After the war, concerns about fairness of employment tests arose, and ethnic/gender biases were evaluated. The APA created Division 14: Industrial and Business Psychology, which eventually evolved into the Society for Industrial and Organizational Psychology (SIOP).

Modern I/O Psychology

Today, I/O psychology is a diverse and deep field of research and practice.

📊 SIOP

The Society for Industrial and Organizational Psychology lists 8,000+ members as a division of the APA.

📈 Job Growth

The Bureau of Labor Statistics projected this profession to have the greatest growth of all job classifications in the 20 years after 2012.

💰 Salary

Master's degree: $80,000+/year. Doctorate: $110,000+/year (Khanna, Medsker, & Ginter, 2012).

Fields of I/O Psychology

Three broad subfields and four main work environments.

Three broad subfields

1. Industrial Psychology

Describes job requirements and assesses individuals for their ability to meet them. Covers employee selection, training, evaluation, and response to evaluations. Also considers legality of discrimination in hiring.

2. Organizational Psychology

Interested in how relationships among employees affect those employees and business performance. Studies worker satisfaction, motivation, commitment, leadership, organizational culture, and social norms. Also covers harassment and workplace violence.

3. Human Factors Psychology

Studies how workers interact with the tools of work and how to design those tools to optimize productivity, safety, and health. From chair-desk-computer fit to complex displays. Known as ergonomics in Europe.

Four main work environments

🎓 Academia
Research universities, teaching, publishing scientific findings about workplace behavior and psychology.
🏛️ Government
Public sector organizations, military, policy development, civil service selection systems.
💼 Consulting
Advising businesses on hiring practices, organizational development, leadership, and culture change.
🏢 Business
Internal HR and organizational development roles within companies — training design, performance management, employee engagement.

Six Key Areas of I/O Psychology

According to Muchinsky (2012), most I/O psychologists work in one of these six major subject areas.

Overview

#AreaCore Focus
1Training & DevelopmentDetermining skills needed for specific jobs; developing and evaluating employee training programs
2Employee SelectionDeveloping selection assessments and screening tests to determine applicant qualifications
3ErgonomicsDesigning procedures and equipment to maximize performance and minimize injury
4Performance ManagementDeveloping assessments and techniques to determine if employees are performing their jobs well
5Work LifeImproving employee satisfaction and maximizing workforce productivity; improving quality of life at work
6Organizational DevelopmentImproving organizations through increasing profits, redesigning products, and improving organizational structure

Detailed breakdown

1 · Training & Development

Professionals in this area determine what types of skills are necessary to perform specific jobs and design, deliver, and evaluate employee training programs. This includes needs assessments, learning design, instructional methods, and measuring training effectiveness.

2 · Employee Selection

This area involves developing employee selection assessments — such as screening tests — to determine if job applicants are qualified for a particular position. Includes psychometric test development, structured interviews, assessment centers, and validation studies to ensure legal compliance.

3 · Ergonomics

The field of ergonomics involves designing procedures and equipment to maximize performance and minimize injury. I/O psychologists in this area study human-machine interaction, workspace design, cognitive workload, and fatigue to create safer, more efficient environments.

4 · Performance Management

I/O psychologists here develop assessments and techniques to determine if employees are doing their jobs well. This encompasses performance appraisal systems, feedback mechanisms, goal setting (e.g., MBO), and performance improvement plans.

5 · Work Life

This area focuses on improving employee satisfaction and maximizing workforce productivity. I/O psychologists may find ways to make jobs more rewarding, design programs that improve workplace quality of life, address work-life balance, burnout, and employee well-being.

6 · Organizational Development

I/O psychologists in this area help improve organizations — often through increasing profits, redesigning products, and improving organizational structure. It includes culture change initiatives, leadership development, team effectiveness, and change management.

Concepts to know

Hawthorne Effect
Increase in performance of individuals who are noticed, watched, or paid attention to by researchers/supervisors. Effect fades when attention fades.
Scientific Management
Taylor's theory: redesign workplaces to increase output and wages. Based on time-and-motion studies. Critics said it exploited workers and assumed workers were lazy.
Ergonomics / Human Factors
Study of machine–human fit. Designing tools and workplaces to optimize productivity, safety, and health.
Group Dynamics
Term coined by Kurt Lewin. The study of group interactions, cooperation, competition, and communication.
Army Alpha Test
WWII-era mental abilities test for literate English-speaking draftees. Developed under Surgeon General's Office.
Army Beta Test
Non-verbal version of Army Alpha for illiterate and non-English-speaking draftees.
APA Division 14
Industrial and Business Psychology division created by the APA; evolved into SIOP.
SIOP
Society for Industrial and Organizational Psychology. A division of the APA with 8,000+ members.
Psychological Corporation
Psychological consulting company founded by James Cattell; still operating today.
Hawthorne Works
Western Electric plant near Chicago; site of Mayo's landmark organizational psychology studies.
