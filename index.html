RA 9262

Anti-VAWC Act of 2004

Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children
Year
2004
Protects
Women & children
Fine (max)
₱300,000
Perpetrator
Partner/spouse
4 Types of Violence (Sec. 3)
Physical Violence
Acts that cause bodily or physical harm.
Sexual Violence
Sexual acts committed against the woman/child, including rape, sexual harassment, forcing sexual activity, and prostitution.
Psychological Violence
Acts causing mental/emotional suffering — intimidation, harassment, stalking, public humiliation, repeated verbal abuse, mental infidelity.
Economic Abuse
Making a woman financially dependent — withdrawing support, preventing work, destroying property, controlling money/properties.
Other Key Definitions
  • Battery — inflicting physical harm resulting in physical and psychological/emotional distress
  • Battered Woman Syndrome — scientifically defined pattern of psychological and behavioral symptoms in women in battering relationships
  • Stalking — knowingly and without lawful justification following or placing the woman/child under surveillance
  • Dating relationship — parties live as husband and wife without marriage, or are romantically involved on a continuing basis (casual acquaintance is excluded)
  • Children — below 18 years of age, or incapable of taking care of themselves; includes biological children and those in her care
Penalties (Sec. 6)
  • Physical harm → follows Revised Penal Code (parricide, murder, homicide); serious injuries → prison mayor
  • Threatening harm → 2 degrees lower than consummated crime (min. arresto mayor)
  • Placing in fear / compelling conduct → arresto mayor
  • Restricting freedom of movement → prision correccional
  • Causing sexual activity without rape → prision mayor
  • Psychological/emotional anguish → prision mayor
  • If committed while pregnant or in presence of a child → maximum period applies
  • Additional fine: ₱100,000 – ₱300,000 + mandatory psychological counseling
Rights of Victims (Sec. 35)
  • To be treated with respect and dignity
  • Legal assistance from PAO / any public legal assistance office
  • Support services from DSWD and LGUs
  • All legal remedies under the Family Code
  • Information about their rights and right to apply for a protection order
RA 7877

Anti-Sexual Harassment Act of 1995

Work, Education & Training Environments
Year
1995
Fine
₱10,000–₱20,000
Prison
1–6 months
Prescription
3 years
Definition
Sexual harassment is committed by a person in authority (employer, supervisor, teacher, coach, etc.) who demands, requests, or requires sexual favor from another — regardless of whether the demand is accepted.
In a Work Environment, SH is committed when:
  • Sexual favor is a condition for hiring, employment, re-employment, or promotion
  • Refusal to grant the favor limits, segregates, or adversely affects employment opportunities
In an Education/Training Environment, SH is committed when:
  • Sexual favor is a condition for passing grades, honors, scholarships, or allowances
  • Sexual advances create an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment for the student/trainee/apprentice
RA 6725

Anti-Discrimination in Employment

Amends Labor Code Art. 135
Prohibited Acts (Art. 135)

  • Favoring a male employee for promotions, training, scholarships solely based on sex

RA 9710

Magna Carta of Women

Comprehensive Women's Human Rights Law
Based on
CEDAW & ICESCR
Oversight body
PCW (Phil. Commission on Women)
GAD
Gender & Development mainstreaming
The MCW is the local translation of CEDAW — it defines gender discrimination, state obligations, substantive equality, and temporary special measures for Filipino women, especially those in marginalized sectors.
Key Rights Guaranteed
  • Protection from all forms of violence (barangay VAW desks, training for gov't personnel)
  • Protection and security during disasters and calamities
  • Participation and representation in civil service, planning bodies, and politics
  • Equal treatment before the law (discriminatory laws to be reviewed/repealed)
  • Equal access to education, scholarships, training; no expulsion due to pregnancy
  • Equal participation in sports
  • Non-discrimination in military, police, and similar services
  • Non-derogatory portrayal in media and film
  • Comprehensive health services covering all life stages (including family planning)
  • 2-month paid gynecological surgery leave (min. 6 months continuous service required)
  • Equal rights in marriage and family relations
Marginalized Sectors Covered
  • Small farmers & rural workers, Fisherfolk, Urban poor
  • Formal & informal economy workers, Migrant workers
  • Indigenous peoples, Moro people
  • Children (under 18), Senior citizens (60+), Persons with disabilities
  • Solo parents (per RA 8972)
Government Responsibilities
  • All agencies (incl. GOCCs, LGUs) must adopt gender mainstreaming
  • Maintain gender statistics & sex-disaggregated databases
  • Commission on Human Rights acts as Gender & Development Ombud
  • Commission on Audit conducts annual audit of GAD budgets
Penalties
  • Gov't violator: civil service sanctions recommended by CHR to CSC and DILG
  • Private violator: liable to pay damages + other legal remedies
  • Violence by State agents = aggravating offense with corresponding higher penalties
RA 7882

Women in Micro & Cottage Business

Assistance to Women Entrepreneurs
Eligibility
Filipino women, 18+
Max Inventory
₱25,000/day
Interest rate
Prime or 12% p.a. (lower)
Penalty
1 yr prison or ₱5,000
Key Provisions
  • Priority loans for women in existing micro/cottage business (≥1 yr with good track record)
  • Business learners certified by TESDA eligible for loans up to ₱25,000 capitalization
  • TESDA provides free technical training
  • DTI Bureau of Small and Medium Business Development is the implementing agency
  • Prohibited: mortgaging loan-funded equipment; refusing/delaying loans without justification
RA 11210

105-Day Expanded Maternity Leave Law

For All Female Workers — Public & Private
Standard leave
105 days (full pay)
Solo mothers
+15 days (full pay)
Optional ext.
+30 days (no pay)
Miscarriage
60 days (full pay)
Key Rules
  • Applies to all female workers regardless of civil status or legitimacy of child
  • Leave must be availed continuously (before or after delivery) — cannot be deferred
  • Granted for every instance of pregnancy, miscarriage, or emergency termination
  • SSS members must have paid ≥3 monthly contributions in the 12 months before the semester of childbirth
  • Employer must advance full payment within 30 days of application; SSS reimburses employer 100%
  • Postnatal leave must be at least 60 days
  • Up to 7 days may be allocated to the child's father or alternate caregiver (within 4th degree of consanguinity)
  • Employer cannot use maternity leave as basis for demotion or termination
Penalties
Fine: ₱20,000 – ₱200,000 and/or imprisonment of 6 years and 1 day to 12 years.
RA 8353

Anti-Rape Law of 1997

Reclassifies Rape as Crime Against Persons
Key Provisions
  • Rape is now classified as a Crime Against Persons (not just against chastity) under the Revised Penal Code
  • Penalty: Reclusion perpetua (Art. 266-B)
  • Effect of Pardon (Art. 266-C): subsequent valid marriage between offended party and offender extinguishes criminal action or penalty
  • Presumptions (Art. 266-D): any overt act of resistance, or incapacity to give valid consent, may be accepted as evidence in prosecution
RA 8505

Rape Victim Assistance & Protection Act of 1998

Rape Crisis Centers in Every Province and City
Initial Fund
₱120M total
DSWD share
₱60M
Others
₱20M each (DOH, DILG, DOJ)
Key Provisions
  • DSWD, DOH, DILG, DOJ + lead NGO must establish a rape crisis center in every province and city
  • Police duty: refer to prosecutor for inquest; arrange counseling and medical services; file a report
  • Closed-door proceedings can be ordered to protect privacy of offended party and accused
  • Rape Shield (Sec. 6): complainant's past sexual conduct/reputation is NOT admissible as evidence unless found material and relevant by the court
RA 8187

Paternity Leave Act of 1996

7 Days for Married Male Employees
Duration
7 days (full pay)
Deliveries covered
First 4 deliveries
Penalty
Fine up to ₱25,000 or 30 days–6 mos imprisonment
Key Rules
  • Only for married male employees in public and private sectors
  • Covers the first four (4) deliveries of the legitimate spouse
  • Includes miscarriage as a qualifying delivery
  • Employee must notify employer of wife's pregnancy and expected delivery date
  • Purpose: enable him to support his wife during recovery and care for the newborn
RA 6949

National Women's Day

March 8 — Working Special Holiday
March 8 of every year is declared a working special holiday known as National Women's Day. Government and private employers must afford employees time to participate in related activities.
RA 10398

National VAWC Consciousness Day

November 25 — Annual Awareness Day
Date
November 25
Budget source
5% of GAD budget
What Must Happen on Nov. 25
  • PIA must allocate ≥1 hour of airtime for VAWC awareness programs
  • Publishers encouraged to feature VAWC issues in print media
  • Schools must organize consciousness-raising activities
  • Gov't & LGU leaders must promote VAWC awareness
Agencies Involved
  • National level: NSO (data), PNP (enforcement), DSWD (welfare), DOLE (labor), DFA (overseas cases), PCW (gender equality), IACAT (trafficking), IAC-VAWC
  • Regional level: RIACAT-VAWC — evaluates local activities and submits annual reports on Nov. 25
RA 7192

Women in Development and Nation Building Act

Equal Partners in National Development
Lead agency
NEDA
Goal
Full equality before the law
Key Provisions
  • Equality in contracts: Women of legal age have equal capacity to act and enter into contracts as men, regardless of civil status
  • Equal club membership: Equal access to social, civic, and recreational clubs and organizations
  • Military schools: Equal opportunities for appointment, admission, training, and commissioning in AFP and PNP schools
  • Voluntary SSS/GSIS/Pag-IBIG: Full-time homemakers (with working spouse's consent) entitled to coverage up to ½ of working spouse's compensation
  • NEDA ensures women's participation in foreign aid, grants, and loans
  • Key areas: gender-responsive education, employment, agriculture, social services, and budgeting

💡 Why These Laws Matter — Conclusion

  • Protection Against Discrimination: Makes it illegal to treat someone unfairly based on gender
  • Access to Opportunities: Removes obstacles in education, jobs, and other fields for women
  • Equal Pay & Fair Labor: Ensures equal pay for the same work and workplace protection
  • Protection from Violence & Harassment: Legal support and protection for victims of gender-based violence
  • Reproductive Rights & Health: Protects bodily autonomy and access to maternal/reproductive health services
  • Social & Cultural Change: Laws shift societal norms toward more equal and respectful attitudes on gender
