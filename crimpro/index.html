Criminal Law vs. Criminal Procedure

Substantive Criminal Law

Defines crimes, treats of their nature, and provides for their appropriate punishment (e.g., Revised Penal Code, Special Penal Laws).

Creates & Defines Rights

Procedural Criminal Law

Provides the mechanism/method by which the sovereign state prosecutes, arrests, tries, and punishes offenders of criminal law.

Provides Method of Enforcement

Rules on Court Testimony & Witnesses

Rules of Evidence

Definition of Court Testimony

Formal, oral, or written statements delivered under oath or affirmation before a court of competent jurisdiction by a witness during trial proceedings to prove or disprove facts in issue.

The 4 Sequential Stages of Examination

1. Direct Examination

Examination-in-chief by the party calling the witness to elicit relevant facts supporting their case.

2. Cross-Examination

By adverse party to test accuracy, truthfulness, credibility, bias, or impeach the witness.

3. Re-Direct Examination

By calling party to explain or clarify matters touched upon during cross-examination.

4. Re-Cross Examination

By adverse party on matters covered in re-direct examination and allowed by court.

Witness Classifications

  • Ordinary Witness: Testifies strictly to facts perceived through their own senses (sight, hearing, etc.). Opinions generally inadmissible.
  • Expert Witness: Testifies giving opinions on matters requiring special skill, knowledge, training, or education (e.g., Forensic Pathologist, Ballistics Expert).

Judicial Affidavit Rule (A.M. No. 12-8-8-SC)

Replaces direct oral testimony in criminal cases where the maximum penalty does not exceed 6 years, or where the accused agrees regardless of penalty.

Key Requirement: Must be executed under oath with attestation by counsel that they conducted or supervised the examination.

Rule 110: Prosecution of Offenses

Rules of Court

How Criminal Actions are Instituted

Offenses Requiring Preliminary Investigation

By filing the complaint with the proper officer for the purpose of conducting the required preliminary investigation (Penalty: at least 4 years, 2 months, 1 day).

Direct Filing in Court / Prosecutor

Filing directly with First Level Courts (MTC/MeTC/MCTC) or with the Office of the Prosecutor in Manila and other chartered cities.

Complaint vs. Information

Feature Complaint (Sec. 3) Information (Sec. 4)
Definition Sworn written statement charging a person with an offense Accusation in writing charging a person with an offense
Subscribed By Offended party, peace officer, or other public officer charged with enforcement Subscribed strictly by the Prosecutor
Oath Requirement Must be subscribed under OATH No oath required from prosecutor (filed under official oath)
Where Filed Prosecutor's Office or Court Filed directly in Court

Crimes Against Chastity (Sec. 5)

Adultery, Concubinage, Seduction, Abduction, and Acts of Lasciviousness cannot be prosecuted except upon a complaint filed by the offended party, parents, grandparents, or guardian.

Special Rule: Pardon by offended party bars prosecution for Adultery & Concubinage if given prior to institution.

Katarungang Pambarangay (KP Law / LGC)

Condition precedent before filing criminal complaint for offenses punishable by imprisonment not exceeding 1 year or fine not exceeding ₱5,000.

Exceptions: Offenses with no private victim, parties reside in different municipalities/cities, accused under detention.

Amendment vs. Substitution of Information (Sec. 14)

Amendment

  • May involve formal or substantial changes.
  • Before Plea: Can amend in form or substance without leave of court.
  • After Plea: Formal amendments allowed only with leave of court and if no prejudice to accused rights. Substantial amendments prohibited.
  • Refers to the same original offense charged or included therein.

Substitution

  • Involves replacing original information with a distinct and new offense.
  • Requires court dismissal of original information upon filing of new one.
  • Only allowed if original information can be dismissed without double jeopardy.
  • Requires new preliminary investigation and new plea entry.

Rule 111: Prosecution of Civil Action

Civil Liability ex delicto

Implied Institution Rule

When a criminal action is instituted, the civil action for recovery of civil liability arising from the offense charged (ex delicto) is deemed instituted with the criminal action unless the offended party:

1. Waives the civil action
2. Reserves right to institute separately
3. Institutes civil action prior to criminal

Independent Civil Actions

Civil actions under Articles 32, 33, 34, and 2176 of the Civil Code proceed independently of the criminal prosecution and require only a preponderance of evidence.

  • Art. 32: Constitutional Rights Violations
  • Art. 33: Defamation, Fraud, Physical Injuries
  • Art. 34: Police Refusal/Failure to Render Aid
  • Art. 2176: Quasi-delict (Negligence)

Prejudicial Question (Sec. 6-7)

An issue involved in a previously instituted civil action which is intimately connected with the issue raised in the subsequent criminal action, the resolution of which determines whether or not the criminal action may proceed.

Requisites: (1) Civil action filed PRIOR to criminal; (2) Issues in civil determine guilt/innocence in criminal.

Rule 112: Preliminary Investigation

A.M. No. 24-02-09-SC Updates

Preliminary Investigation Standard

New Jurisprudence Quantum

An inquiry or proceeding to determine whether there is sufficient ground to engender a well-founded belief that a crime has been committed and the respondent is probably guilty thereof and should be held for trial.

Quantum of Evidence in PI (A.M. No. 24-02-09-SC / Dept. Circular 020)

"Prima Facie Evidence with Reasonable Certainty of Conviction"

Requires prosecutors to assess not merely probable cause but whether admissible evidence exists to sustain a conviction beyond reasonable doubt at trial.

Comparison: PI vs. Custodial Investigation vs. Inquest

Parameter Preliminary Investigation Custodial Investigation Inquest Proceeding
Nature Executive/Judicial Inquiry Police Questioning of Suspect Summary Inquiry by Prosecutor
Status of Accused Usually not under detention In custody / deprived of freedom Warrantlessly arrested person
Conducting Officer State/City/Provincial Prosecutor Law Enforcement / Police Officers Inquest Prosecutor
Coverage Requirement Offenses penalized by at least 4y 2m 1d Any interrogation after custody Lawful warrantless arrest cases

Rule 113: Arrest

Constitutional Guarantees

Definition & Essential Requisites

Arrest is the taking of a person into custody that he may be bound to answer for the commission of an offense. It is made by an actual restraint of a person, or by his submission to the custody of the person making the arrest.

Section 5: Lawful Warrantless Arrests

1. In Flagrante Delicto (Sec. 5a)

When, in the officer's presence, the person to be arrested has committed, is actually committing, or is attempting to commit an offense.

Requisites: Overt act + Personal knowledge by officer.
2. Hot Pursuit (Sec. 5b)

When an offense has just been committed, and officer has probable cause based on personal knowledge of facts/circumstances that suspect committed it.

Requisites: Offense JUST committed + Probable cause on personal knowledge.
3. Escaped Prisoner (Sec. 5c)

When person to be arrested is a prisoner who has escaped from a penal establishment or place where serving sentence or temporarily confined.

May be arrested anytime and anywhere without warrant.

Warrant Types

  • Regular Warrant of Arrest: Issued by judge upon personal determination of probable cause. Validity lasts until executed or recalled.
  • Bench Warrant: Issued directly by judge from the bench for contempt or failure of accused to appear during trial.
  • Alias Warrant: Re-issued warrant when original warrant was unserved or returned without execution.

Miranda Rights & RA 7438 Statutory Safeguards

  • Right to remain silent.
  • Right to have competent & independent counsel of own choice.
  • Right to be informed of these rights.
  • Right to be visited by immediate family, counsel, or medical doctor.
Waivers of Miranda rights must be in WRITING and in the presence of COUNSEL.

Rule 114: Bail

Constitutional Right

Definition & Essential Prerequisite

Security given for the release of a person in custody of the law, furnished by him or a bondsman, to guarantee his appearance before any court as required.

Custody of Law Rule: Bail cannot be applied for or granted unless the applicant is either under arrest or has voluntarily surrendered to court jurisdiction.
1. Corporate Surety

Bail bond furnished by a licensed insurance or bonding company authorized by SC.

2. Property Bond

An undertaking constituted as lien on real property given as security for amount of bail.

3. Cash Deposit

Deposit of specified cash sum with city/municipal treasurer or clerk of court.

4. Recognizance

Obligation of record entered into before court by a responsible person/entity.

Bail: Matter of Right vs. Discretionary

Bail as a Matter of Right

  • Before or after conviction by MTC / MeTC / MCTC.
  • Before conviction by RTC for offenses NOT punishable by death, reclusion perpetua, or life imprisonment.

Bail as Discretionary

  • Upon conviction by RTC of offense NOT punishable by death, reclusion perpetua, or life imprisonment.
  • Before conviction by RTC for capital offenses when evidence of guilt is NOT strong.
