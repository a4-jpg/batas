Introduction & Background ARC & Key Institutions
Source This lesson adapts the American Red Cross (2012) Job Responsibilities during Disasters framework. In the Philippines, its equivalent is the Philippine Red Cross (PRC).
🇺🇸

American Red Cross (ARC)

A non-profit, independent, humanitarian organization that provides emergency assistance, disaster relief, and disaster preparedness education in the United States.

🇵🇭

Philippine Red Cross (PRC)

The Philippine equivalent of the ARC. Performs the same mandate — emergency assistance, disaster relief, and disaster preparedness education — in the Philippine context.

Key Disaster & Mental Health Institutions
  • 🏥 Department of Health (DOH) — Government health authority overseeing public health including mental health services during disasters
  • 🛡️ Philippine Disaster Resilience Foundation (PDRF) — Private sector-led organization for disaster resilience efforts
  • ❤️ Philippine Red Cross (PRC) — Humanitarian operations, relief services, and volunteer deployment
  • 📋 National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) — National coordinating body for all disaster risk reduction and management efforts
  • 🎓 Academic / Research Units — Universities and research centers contributing expertise and evidence-based practices
  • 🏘️ Local and Community-Based Service Providers — Grassroots organizations delivering on-the-ground psychosocial support
  • 🏢 Private Institutions — Corporate and private sector partners contributing resources and specialized services

Part 1 — 8-Step Process DMH Worker Responsibilities during Disaster
Overview DMH workers follow an 8-step process from the moment they are deployed to the time they leave the relief operation. Each step carries specific duties and expectations.
Step 1

In-Processing (Registration)

All DMH workers must complete registration tasks before deployment. This is a non-negotiable first responsibility.

Step 2

Orientation

The DMH supervisor ensures every worker or volunteer is properly oriented before they are deployed to their work assignment.

Step 3

Job Assignments

Workers receive assignments from a DMH supervisor or site manager, based on operational needs and individual skills.

Step 4

Gathering Needed Information

Workers must understand specific job expectations and the chain of command at their job site before starting work.

Step 5

Acquiring Needed Supplies

Workers must obtain all supplies necessary to perform their functions effectively during the operation.

Step 6

Using PsySTART

Workers use the PsySTART Aggregated Worksheet to record client risk factors and contacts with clients and responders.

Step 7

Work Performance Evaluation

Workers review the evaluation form and discuss performance expectations with their supervisor before beginning work.

Step 8

Out-Processing

Workers collaborate closely with supervisors to complete all transition tasks before officially leaving the relief operation.

Step 2 Deep Dive: What Orientation Covers
  • The nature of the disaster itself
  • Service delivery plans and status of health services, sheltering, feeding, and all other response components
  • The affected population and any special considerations that help workers better assist clients
  • Expectations for workers, including the need to be flexible, patient, and attentive to self-care throughout the relief operation
Step 3 Deep Dive: Basis of Job Assignments
How Assignments Are Decided Assignments should be based on both organizational need and the worker's personal development path within the Red Cross.
  • Need for DMH coverage at service delivery sites
  • Optimal opportunities for job satisfaction and career development within the Red Cross
  • Human resources system position (e.g., DMH service associate)
  • Disaster training and disaster experience
  • Leadership and communication skills and experience
  • Employment experience and worker interests
  • Worker promotion and development plans
Step 4 Deep Dive: Important Information to Gather
  • Name and contact information of your immediate supervisor
  • Name and contact info for the DMH lead or designee at DRO headquarters
  • Specific assignment address and directions
  • Name and contact info for the administrative supervisor or manager of the service delivery site
  • Hours of operation for the site
Step 8 Deep Dive: Out-Processing Tasks
  • Making travel arrangements for returning home
  • Ensuring any assigned rental vehicle is returned
  • Returning any cell phone assigned to you on the DRO
  • Reviewing the assigned Disaster Staff Card funds to ensure sufficient funds for the return trip
  • Arranging a performance review with your immediate technical supervisor or site supervisor
  • Planning for sufficient time to complete all of the above tasks

Part 2 — Step 5 & 6 Details Typical Supplies & PsySTART System
Why Supplies Matter DMH workers must secure all materials needed for their function. The type and quantity of supplies will vary based on the disaster's scale and site assignment.
Material / Supply Situation for Use
📱 Cell Phone Assigned to each team, not to individual workers. Shared device for team communication.
🚗 Service Vehicles (Rental Cars) Available when necessary for travel to service delivery sites. Cars are shared and may be reassigned to other activities at any time.
📋 PsySTART Aggregated Worksheet & Supervisor Daily Summary Used by field staff and supervisors to aggregate client risk factors and record contacts with clients and responders.
📄 Client Consent to Share Information Used for the informed consent process when working with clients and Red Cross staff.
📚 Educational Materials DMH print materials available to assist both clients and responders in coping with disasters.
🖊️ Basic Office Supplies Paper, pens, and other basics are usually available at the DRO.
🗺️ Maps and Directions Made available to help workers navigate to and from service delivery work sites.
PsySTART® MH Triage Quick Guide
What is PsySTART? PsySTART is a mental health triage system used by DMH workers to systematically identify disaster survivors who may need immediate or elevated mental health support. Workers stop and assess whenever a risk factor is present.
Triage Factor How to Assess Triage Category
Expressed thought or intent to harm self/others Do you feel like you want to hurt yourself or hurt anyone else since the incident? 🟣 STOP — Triage Purple
Felt or expressed extreme fear or panic During or after the incident, felt like was "losing control," "couldn't think," or "going crazy" 🔴 STOP — Triage Red
Trapped or delayed evacuation Was trapped for a long period of time 🔴 STOP — Triage Red
Expressed felt direct threat to life of self or family member Says felt like would/will die OR felt like family was going to die 🔴 STOP — Triage Red
Saw/heard death or serious injury of other (include parent, child, family member, stranger) Indicates saw others dead, burned, mutilated, blood, or heard screams/cries for help 🔴 STOP — Triage Red
Death of one parent, child, or family member Anyone in family died 🔴 STOP — Triage Red
Family member / care giver currently separated, missing, or unaccounted for Any family missing 🔴 STOP — Triage Red
Child separated from all caregivers Unaccompanied minor 🔴 STOP — Triage Red
History of mental health care Have you been diagnosed for or received treatment for any mental health problems? 🟡 STOP — Triage Yellow
Confirmed exposure/contamination OR concerned about possible exposures Concerned about perception of being exposed to toxic/CBRN 🟡 STOP — Triage Yellow
NONE of the above No triage factors identified 🟢 STOP — Triage Green
Action Guide 🟣 Purple → Immediately contact site supervisor and DMH or call 911. 🔴 Red → Contact DMH as soon as possible. 🟡 Yellow → Contact DMH at the end of your shift. 🟢 Green → No further action needed at this time.

Part 3 — DMH Supervisor Role Responsibilities of DMH Supervisors
Who is the DMH Supervisor? The DMH Supervisor oversees, supports, and ensures the proper orientation of DMH workers or volunteers during crisis operations, and provides critical psychosocial interventions when needed.
8 Core Responsibilities of DMH Supervisors
Responsibility 1

DMH Supervisor Tasks

Performing all general supervisory duties to maintain operational quality and worker wellbeing.

Responsibility 2

Adjusting Worker Assignments

DMH assignments may change. Flexibility is a critical skill for all DMH staff.

Responsibility 3

Arranging Days Off

Supervisors must track duty hours and provide days off to help workers balance life and work.

Responsibility 4

Reporting to DMH Leader

Report team successes and challenges so that DRO leadership has accurate operational information.

Responsibility 5

Regular Communication with Staff

Hold regular meetings for positive feedback, updates, and timely resolution of worker concerns.

Responsibility 6

Recordkeeping via PsySTART

Complete and submit the PsySTART Supervisor Daily Summary at the end of each 24-hour reporting period.

Responsibility 7

PsySTART for Service Delivery

The Supervisor Daily Summary shows totals of client risk factors and contacts recorded by all supervised responders.

Responsibility 8

Conducting Performance Evaluations

It is the supervisor's responsibility to complete performance evaluations for each responder assigned to them.

Responsibility 1 Expanded: Supervisor Task List
  • Ensuring the quality of client care
  • Implementing the service delivery plan
  • Mitigating worker risks of compassion fatigue or secondary traumatization
  • Providing useful support, feedback, and direction
  • Clearly explaining expectations for job performance
  • Providing technical supervision related to DMH interventions
  • Answering questions about Red Cross DMH policies and procedures
  • Ensuring that each worker receives a day off within every 7 days of work
  • Ensuring the safety of each worker on the disaster operation
  • Providing updates on service delivery plans
  • Personally thanking each DMH worker for his or her volunteer service
PsySTART Supervisor Daily Summary Monitors Number of DMH contacts per responder per site · Number and type of PsySTART risk factors identified · Sites with clients carrying greater numbers of risk factors · Responders working with high-risk-factor clients who may need extra support

Part 4 — DMH Leader (Disaster Site Manager) Responsibilities of DMH Leaders
Who is the DMH Leader? Also known as the Disaster Site Manager. Responsible for planning, coordinating, and leading immediate DMH response efforts across the entire operation.
5 Core Responsibilities of DMH Leaders
Area 1

Organizing DMH Service Delivery

Establishing a leadership team, mentoring DMH workers, planning service delivery, and setting up headquarters space.

Area 2

Staffing DMH

Ensuring sufficient, qualified staff are recruited, oriented, and assigned appropriately across all service delivery sites.

Area 3

Supporting DMH Services

Obtaining and maintaining all materials and supplies needed for the DMH response throughout the operation.

Area 4

Partnering with Groups

Building and maintaining collaborative relationships with all internal activity leads, operations management, and external community partners.

Area 5

Scaling Down the DMH Activity

Managing the handover of activities to local community DMH workers and drafting a written transition plan.

Area 1 Deep Dive: Common DMH Leadership Positions
Operations Lead

Operations Lead

  • Acts as direct supervisor for DMH supervisors at service delivery sites or within a designated affected area
  • Screens, orients, and assigns incoming DMH responders
  • Oversees materials and resources
  • Assumes duties of the DMH lead during days off or absences
  • Assigns a DMH worker to oversee DMH referrals in the Client Assistance System (CAS)
Community Liaison Lead

Community Liaison Lead

  • Coordinates with local community mental health organizations, Medical Reserve Corps, and other MH partners
  • Assesses the community's cultural composition and ensures culturally-appropriate service delivery
  • Identifies local crisis hotlines, emergency facilities, mental health resources, and commitment laws
  • Assesses and works with affected school systems
  • Attends community meetings and coordinates media interviews with Public Affairs
Staff Mental Health Lead

Staff Mental Health Lead

  • Has primary responsibility for staff mental health on the entire disaster operation
  • Works closely with Staff Wellness and/or Staff Relations on staff health and performance-related issues
  • Provides and oversees mental health out-processing support
  • Provides the mental health portion of general staff orientations at response headquarters
  • Carries the DMH after-hours phone, or arranges rotation of this responsibility
Administrative Assistant

Administrative Assistant

  • Provides primary response headquarters DMH phone coverage
  • Registers newly arriving DMH responders and updates the DMH "job book"
  • Sets up schedules for community volunteers
  • Collects and reports statistics using the PsySTART system
  • Fields and distributes information that comes to DMH at response headquarters
  • Performs general office management tasks
Area 1 Continued: Mentoring DMH Workers
Why Mentoring Matters Mentoring is a vital role of any DMH Leader. It supports responder career development through a structured, sustained process for facilitating responder growth and promotion within the Red Cross.
Area 3: Common Supplies Needed by DMH Leader
  • ⚡ Accessibility to electrical supply and equipment
  • 📄 Administrative and client forms
  • 🌐 Internet connectivity
  • 🚗 Service vehicle for transportation
  • 💰 Source of funds for operational expenses
Area 5: Scaling Down the DMH Activity
  • The DMH Leader recognizes the need for handing over activities from the relief operation to local community DMH workers and volunteers
  • Leftover materials are typically left with the affected chapters to help them restock their pre-disaster supplies
  • DMH leadership must provide a written transition plan to the job director specifying what DMH needs are anticipated after the DRO closes and who will be responsible for continuing DMH support

Lesson 9 at a Glance Key Points Summary

📌 LESSON 9 — KEY TAKEAWAYS

Source Framework
Adapted from American Red Cross (2012) Job Responsibilities during Disasters. Philippine equivalent: Philippine Red Cross (PRC).
Key Institutions
DOH, PDRF, PRC, NDRRMC, Academic/Research Units, Local/Community-based providers, and Private Institutions.
8 Worker Steps
In-Processing → Orientation → Job Assignment → Gathering Info → Acquiring Supplies → Using PsySTART → Performance Evaluation → Out-Processing
Registration Rule
All DMH workers must complete registration tasks BEFORE deployment. This is non-negotiable.
PsySTART Triage Colors
🟣 Purple = imminent harm → call 911. 🔴 Red = high risk → contact DMH immediately. 🟡 Yellow = moderate risk → report at end of shift. 🟢 Green = no risk factors identified.
DMH Supervisor Role
Oversees, supports, and orients workers. Key duties: quality care, staff safety, flexibility in assignments, PsySTART reporting, and performance evaluations. Workers must get 1 day off per every 7 days worked.
DMH Leader Role
Also called Disaster Site Manager. Responsible for organizing service delivery, staffing, acquiring supplies, internal/external partnerships, and scaling down the operation.
Flexibility Rule
Flexibility is a critical skill for all DMH staff. Workers may be asked to assume responsibilities outside their preferences or expectations.
Scaling Down
DMH Leader must prepare a written transition plan for the job director specifying post-DRO DMH needs and responsible parties for continuing support.
