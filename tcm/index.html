What is TCM?

Therapeutic Community Modality — definition, components, and purpose

Word-by-word breakdown

Breaking Down the Term

Therapeutic

Related to healing or treatment. A person suffering from a disorder or mental illness undergoes a therapeutic process to be healed or treated.

Community

TCM is a group-based approach — healing and treatment procedures are done within a group. Other people are included in the treatment process.

Modality

From the root word "mode" — refers to a mode of procedures. The specific method or procedure used in the treatment process.

Official Definition

Full Definition of TCM

Definition (Lecture)A self-help, social learning treatment model used for clients with problems of drug abuse and other behavioral problems such as alcoholism, stealing, and other antisocial tendencies. An environment that helps people get help while helping others.

It is a treatment environment where the interaction of its members are designed to be therapeutic within the context of norms that require each member to play the dual role of client and therapist.

⚠️ Key Exam Point: TCM is also called "TC" in jail institutions. It is classified as a treatment approach that is group-based, aimed at behavioral and psychological intervention for rehabilitation.
Core Characteristics

Three Core Characteristics

  1. TCM is a treatment approach
  2. It is group-based in nature
  3. Its purpose is behavioral and psychological intervention — to help inmates change from law violators into law-abiding citizens of the community
Why "Self-Help"?

The Self-Help Principle

Although TC is a group-based approach, it is still called self-help because treatment depends on the individual participation, voluntariness, and interest of the person. Even if a person joins a group, if they have no genuine interest in being healed or changing, the treatment becomes ineffective.

TCM in Jail Programs

TCM as Part of the IWDP

The Therapeutic Community Modality is one of the activities under the Inmates Welfare and Development Program (IWDP). It falls specifically under the Behavioral Management/Modification Program.

Board Exam Note: The purpose of TCM is rehabilitation only — not retribution, not deterrence. Remember: TCM = Rehabilitation.

Punishment

Justifications of punishment and their relevance to TCM

Definition

What is Punishment?

DefinitionPunishment is the redress that the state takes against an offending member of society that usually involves pain and suffering. It is the instrument used by the state against a person who violated the law.
Five Justifications of Punishment

Justifications of Punishment

#JustificationKey WordExplanation
1RetributionVengeance / Individual RevengeThe person deserves to be punished. It is a form of individual revenge — he committed a crime, therefore he deserves punishment.
2Expiation / AtonementGroup VengeancePurpose is to appease the offended public or group. Crime offends not just the personal victim but the community in general. Also called group vengeance.
3DeterrenceSpecific & GeneralSpecific deterrence — the criminal himself is deterred from committing again after being punished. General deterrence — the public is deterred upon seeing someone punished.
4Incapacitation & ProtectionParalyze / ProtectThe public is protected if the offender is held in conditions where he cannot harm others. Prevents future victimization by removing the offender from the community.
5Reformation / RehabilitationChange / ReformThe latest justification. Changing the offender from a bad guy into a good one; from irresponsible to responsible; from unproductive to productive. This is the purpose of TCM.
Board Exam Note: Reformation/Rehabilitation is the latest justification of punishment. TCM's purpose falls only under this justification.

Correction Agencies

Fragmented structure of the Philippine correction pillar

The Fragmentation Problem

Why the System is Fragmented

The correction agencies in the Philippines are under different supervisory agencies, though they perform similar work. This causes limited collaboration between agencies and ununified programs. Ideally, one supervisory agency should oversee all correction agencies.

BJMP Programs

Inmates Welfare & Development Program — Core & Support Services

Core Programs of the BJMP

Core Programs (IWDP)

The Inmates Welfare and Development Program (IWDP) consists of the following core programs:

1. Provisions of Basic Needs

All PDLs (Persons Deprived of Liberty) under custody are provided with:

  • Three meals a day (breakfast, lunch, supper)
  • Adequate supply of potable water
  • Uniform and basic clothing
  • Shelter/housing within the facility
2. Health Services

Physical and mental health care during confinement:

  • Health education for maintaining good health
  • Free counseling services
  • Free medical consultation (especially for diabetes, hypertension)
  • Regular health monitoring
  • Medicine provided — subject to availability
3. Educational Program

Led by the Department of Education (DepEd) through the Alternative Learning System (ALS). ALS teachers go into jail institutions. PDLs who were not elementary or high school graduates can earn their diploma while incarcerated.

4. Skills Training & Enhancement Program

Partner agency: TESDA (Technical Education and Skills Development Authority). PDLs can earn National Certificate 1 (NC1) or NC2 — a certification proving they are skilled in a specific area (e.g., welding, food & beverage, agriculture). Purpose: increase employability upon release.

5. Livelihood Program

Income-generating activities for PDLs (e.g., making handicrafts, lanterns, rags). Capital comes from BJMP-funded projects or the common fund of a group of PDLs. The Unit Welfare and Development Officer (UWDO) facilitates the sale of products — they are the market facilitator.

6. Behavioral Management / Modification Program

This is where Therapeutic Community Modality (TC) is classified. Purpose: to manage and modify behaviors of PDLs with the goal of positively changing their thinking and behavior through structured group process. The TC activity is conducted in small groups (5–10 PDLs) in the early morning on specific days. PDLs share past experiences, frustrations, and violations, and counsel each other.

7. Interfaith / Religious Activity Program

PDLs are provided with the opportunity to practice their faith without discrimination — whether Adventist, Catholic, Born Again, Iglesia ni Cristo, Muslim, etc. Based on testimonies and research, this is considered the most effective program in changing behavior from a law violator to a law-abiding citizen.

Religious Volunteer Organizations (RVOs) participate and are regulated and managed accordingly.

8. Cultural and Sports Activity Program

Includes chess, basketball, singing, theater, drama, and art workshops. Purpose: to reduce boredom inside jail facilities. Subject to rules and regulations. (In practice, many facilities lack space for these activities due to overcrowding.)

9. Paralegal Program

The paralegal officer assists PDLs to ensure speedy investigation and trial. Purpose: to reduce overcrowding — faster resolution of cases may result in acquittal or dismissal.

10. E-Dalaw (Electronic Visit)

An alternative to traditional face-to-face visitation. Enables PDLs to connect with family through supervised video call and chat (e.g., Messenger, Zoom). Not face-to-face — done through a device and camera.

BuCor / ViCore Programs

Reformation programs of the Bureau of Corrections

Overview

BuCor is Under the DOJ

The Bureau of Corrections (BuCor / ViCore) is under the Department of Justice (DOJ). It handles all convicted individuals with sentences of 3 years and 1 day and above. Its programs are similar to the BJMP's but are managed by different directorates.

The Six Reformation Programs

Phases of TC in BJMP

The four phases of Therapeutic Community Modality

Overview

The Four Phases

Phase 1: Entry & Orientation→ Phase 2: Primary Treatment→ Phase 3: Pre-Reentry→ Phase 4: Reentry
Phase Details

Phase 1 — Entry & Orientation

Phase 1Upon commitment, a resident is placed at the Reception and Diagnostic Room for orientation. A series of examinations are conducted to identify physical, social, and psychological status.

During Phase 1, the PDL is acquainted with:

  • The TC program itself
  • The rules and norms of the community (written and unwritten)
  • The philosophy of the TC
  • The staff and members of the community
  • The tools of the house and job functions
  • The TC hierarchy

Phase 2 — Primary Treatment

After Phase 1, the PDL moves to primary treatment where they must learn:

  • Proper use of different tools to address personal issues and shape behavior
  • Managing own feelings and learning how to express self appropriately
  • Learning to follow the rules and norms of the community
  • Maximizing participation in activities
  • Learning to trust the environment by disclosing self to the community
  • Developing positive coping skills for difficult life situations
  • Enhancing educational and vocational skills to become productive

Phase 3 — Pre-Reentry

"Pre" means before reentry — the PDL is about to be released. Focus areas in this phase:

  • Rebuilding social and family ties — because the commission of a crime may have broken family relationships
  • Going up the ladder of hierarchy by showing leadership skills
  • Realization of full potential to be a productive member of society
  • Mapping out of plans — strategizing for life after release

Phase 4 — Reentry

The PDL is now ready to be released back to society having demonstrated adequate self-control and discipline.

  • Preparing for life outside jail
  • Focus on becoming a productive citizen
  • Job hunting plans
  • Rebuilding family ties and relationships
After Care (Part of Phase 4)After care is an out-patient program requiring clients to report twice a week to an outreach center. They attend group sessions to ensure adjustment to life outside jail and to reduce recidivism.
Pre-Integration Referral System

Inmates Pre-Integration Referral System

Establishes linkages to ensure re-integration opportunities for released detainees. The referral system links released PDLs to:

  • Employment opportunities
  • Vocational training
  • Financial assistance
  • Local government units and business institutions

BuCor Reception & Diagnostics

General process of committing a prisoner and the DRD procedures

General Process in BuCor

General Commitment Process

Former vs. New NameThe former name RDC (Reception and Diagnostic Center) is now DRD (Directorate for Reception and Diagnostics). Update your review materials accordingly!
  1. Reception — BuCor shall undertake reception of inmates through the Directorate for Reception and Diagnostics (DRD)
  2. Security Classification — Provide basic needs and security through the Security and Operations Directorate (minimum / medium / maximum security)
  3. Reformation — Administer reformation programs through its Reformation Directorates
  4. Pre-Release / Reintegration — Prepare inmates for integration to mainstream society through the Directorate for External Relations (DER) — formerly the External Relations Division
Reception Procedures

Reception — Required Documents

BuCor through its penal establishments shall receive prisoners from competent authority upon presentation of:

  • Mittimus / Commitment Order — signed by the judge, ordering the jail/prison officer to commit the person identified in the document
  • Information and Court Decision
  • Certification of Detention
  • Certification of No Pending Case
  • Certification of Non-Appeal
  • Provincial Form 35 — the Inmate Data Sheet
Note: A female offender shall be received only at the Correctional Institution for Women (CIW). She cannot be placed in the DRD for male offenders.
Diagnostics Objectives

Diagnostics — What is Assessed?

The DRD is responsible for conducting classification of inmates. The core diagnostic objective is to determine the inmate's reform ability. This includes:

  • Interest, skills, and talents
  • Physical, social, demographic, and other behavioral trait assessments
  • Spiritual assessment
  • Security classification
  • Type of suitable program for each inmate

Results serve as reference for the DRD in preparing individual inmate reformation treatment programs. Programs must be tailored — one size does not fit all.

Classification Categories

Segregation

Segregation Scheme

After diagnostics, PDLs are segregated according to:

  • Security classification (minimum / medium / maximum)
  • Nature of crimes committed (crimes against persons, against property, against national security, against public order, etc.)
