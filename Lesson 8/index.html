Core Concept

Force Health Protection

Promotes worker resilience Takes place in ALL relief operation settings Self-care prevents compassion fatigue Helps workers & supervisors overcome obstacles to self-care
DMH provides support during 3 Deployment Phases
Phase 01

Pre-Deployment

After staff are assigned to a job and before they leave their home.

Phase 02

During Deployment

While the worker is actively engaged in disaster operations.

Phase 03

Post-Deployment

When the worker begins making arrangements to return home and after leaving the operation.

Phase 1 Pre-Deployment Support
Key Concern With the emphasis on rapid response, pre-deployment preparation and onsite training may be shortchanged. Staff may sometimes deploy when not emotionally prepared or distracted by family needs.
⚠️
⚠ Challenges for Workers
  • Making travel and other arrangements
  • Local disaster personal impact
  • Traveling logistics
  • Anticipation of the unknown
  • Difficulty accessing accurate info & resources
  • In-processing delays
  • "Hurry up and wait"
→ DMH Supports & Services
  • Interviewing (screening) for appropriateness for deployment
  • Building resilience
  • PFA training
  • "Coping with Disaster: Preparing for a Disaster Assignment" brochure
6 steps Stress Management Suggestions Before Deploying
Step 01

Gather Information

Learn conditions at the disaster site so you can bring appropriate clothing and supplies.

Step 02

Gather Self-Care Items

Bring sufficient medication to last several days beyond your expected deployment length.

Step 03

Make Arrangements

Establish with your employer how work will be managed and arrange care for family members and pets.

Step 04

Complete Paperwork

Complete all necessary paperwork at your chapter before departing.

Step 05

Track Deployment

Check for deployment updates immediately before departing and again upon arrival at your destination.

Step 06

Set Up Communication

Provide family with your chapter phone number so they can access emergency communication services.

Phase 2 During Deployment Support
Mindset Successfully managing disaster relief work requires workers to take care of themselves before, during, and after their assignments. Disasters can bring strong emotions even among experienced workers — but we all have resilience.
⚡
⚠ Challenges for Workers
  • Environmental exposures
  • Work-related exposures
  • Managing common stress reactions
  • Burnout
  • Compassion fatigue
→ DMH Supports & Services
  • Staff orientation: strategies to reduce stress
  • Self-care and coping strategies
  • Monitoring environmental and work-related stressors; triage of at-risk staff
  • Constructive feedback to activity leads for reducing stressors
  • Collaborating with Staff Wellness when appropriate
8 strategies Resilience-Building Strategies
Strategy 01

Stay Safe

Attend all work orientations and briefings; follow all safety precautions and guidelines.

Strategy 02

Prioritize Your Work

Stay focused on what you're accomplishing; don't let what remains to be done overwhelm you.

Strategy 03

Make Self-Care Priority #1

Assert yourself and ask for help if you are uncomfortable with your assignment.

Strategy 04

Stay Connected

Connect with other workers. If inexperienced, partnering with someone else can be especially helpful.

Strategy 05

Maintain a Hopeful Outlook

Regularly remind yourself: you are providing for survivors' needs and helping them recover.

Strategy 06

Manage Relationships Professionally

On assignment you will work with teams and supervisors you may have never met before.

Strategy 07

Use Calming Strategies

Consider usual strategies you use to relax or calm yourself and how they can help on assignment.

Strategy 08

Watch for Training Opportunities

Feeling confident about your disaster work skills contributes to your sense of strength and resilience.

Phase 3 Post-Deployment Support
Why it matters Disaster workers often experience the sights, smells, and sounds of disaster just like the community. MH support after disaster aims to assist workers with coping, long-term well-being, and personal growth.
🏠
⚠ Challenges for Workers
  • Transitioning back to pre-deployment lives and routines
  • Incorporating the deployment experience into daily life in a meaningful way
→ DMH Supports & Services
  • Providing opportunities to discuss experiences
  • Normalizing post-deployment reactions
  • Building resilience
  • Providing post-event triage and information about referral resources
Goals & Principles of Post-Deployment DMH Support
2 Goals (1) Help workers successfully transition back to pre-deployment lives and routines. (2) Help them begin to incorporate their deployment experience into their lives in a meaningful way.
  • Participation in post-deployment support should always be voluntary
  • Usually offered by DMH at the end of a relief operation, but should also be offered at the home chapter after deployments
  • Time and place of contact should be convenient and suitable for the disaster worker
  • Support will most typically be provided in an individual one-to-one format
  • When provided in a group, it should be psychoeducational and discussion-oriented
  • Post-deployment contacts should NOT be debriefings and should not specifically aim to elicit workers' feelings
5 Objectives of Post-Deployment Intervention
  • Offer workers an opportunity to talk and ask questions about their deployment
  • Prioritize staff with significant exposure-based risk factors, sub-threshold PTSD, and/or substance abuse to identify those at risk for chronic reactions
  • Provide information that assists in identifying, anticipating, and normalizing post-deployment reactions
  • Provide information to help recognize and build resilience — the worker's ability to bounce back after difficult experiences
  • Establish an understanding of when to seek additional support and provide information about referral resources

Mental Health Emergencies Worker Distress
Key Fact Disaster workers may become distressed for many reasons — not just from the disaster itself. DMH workers must be ready to respond.
Personal crisis while on assignment
Reactions to disaster aftermath & working conditions
Exacerbation of pre-existing conditions
Conflicts with other workers
Being informed of a crisis at home
Exposure to same risk factors as clients (living in disaster-affected area)
Problems the same as among the general population
When Meeting Distressed Workers, DMH Workers: Provide support to the responder · Assess mental health needs and emotional fitness for work · Collaborate with Staff Wellness and Staff Relations and make recommendations as appropriate
MH Emergency — Disposition Recommendations
⏸️

Time Off

Worker given time off to recover before continuing duties.

🔄

Reassignment

Transfer to another activity or assignment setting.

🕊️

Compassionate Release

Released from relief operation with no restriction — may return in the future.

🚨

Immediate Release

Released with recommendation to not be assigned to future operations without further counseling or treatment.

Process Note In MH emergencies, Staff Wellness leads. DMH makes its final recommendation to the technical lead, who consults with Staff Wellness and Staff Relations. OM is notified of all recommendations.

Admin Procedures — PAP Code of Ethics 2022 Confidentiality
Why Admin Procedures Matter Administrative procedures provide an objective set of rules by which DMH professionals and workers are governed. They ensure workers are held accountable for actions that deviate from procedures.
Definition

Confidentiality

The ethical and legal duty to protect sensitive, private, or personal information from unauthorized access, disclosure, or theft. Ensures data shared in trust remains private.

Core Duty

Maintaining It

Safeguard any client information regardless of the medium. Ensure it is secured and not placed in areas or devices easily accessible to unqualified persons.

Recording

Consent Required

Obtain consent from clients or legal representatives before recording voices or images. Explain all anticipated uses of recordings before the session.

Limitations of Confidentiality
  • Clients must be told before sessions how their information may be used
  • Information may be released only after careful consideration — in cases of imminent danger or when required by court
  • For electronic transmissions (fax, email), clients must be warned of privacy risks; use safeguards like encryption and passwords
7 Rules on Disclosures
  • Ensure disclosed information is not misused or misinterpreted
  • Share confidential information only with client/legal consent, unless prohibited by law
  • Disclose to the source of referral only; if self-referral, require written permission from the client
  • Without consent, disclosure allowed only when mandated/permitted by law (e.g., providing services, preventing harm)
  • Limit disclosure strictly to what is necessary
  • Releases to schools, agencies, or organizations must be supervised by qualified practitioners only
  • Information gathered within institutions is confidential and requires management approval for release
Other Use of Confidential Info Do NOT reveal client information in writings, lectures, seminars, classroom discussions, research, publications, or social media — UNLESS: written consent is given, disclosure is legally authorized, or reasonable steps are taken to disguise the client's identity.
Consultation Rule Do not share client-identifying details with colleagues unless consent is given or disclosure is unavoidable. When seeking second opinions, limit shared information strictly to what is necessary.

PAP Code of Ethics 2022 Record Keeping & Informed Consent
Core Purpose Maintaining client records preserves a written history of the therapy process and describes actions taken by the specialist. INFORMED CONSENT = CLIENT'S PROTECTION
5 Reasons We Create and Keep Records
  • Facilitate provision of services by ourselves or other professionals
  • Allow for replication and evaluation of our research
  • Meet institutional requirements
  • Ensure accuracy of billing and payments
  • Ensure compliance with relevant laws
  • Each contact with the client should be documented along with the nature of the contact and type of services given
  • Records should include informed consent and confidentiality waiver
  • In the Philippine context, clients have the right to access their clinical records — unless revealing such information would cause harm to the client's health or put others at risk

DSWD Financial Assistance
Key Agency During a disaster, people turn to the government for financial assistance. The DSWD (Department of Social Welfare and Development) is mandated to provide financial assistance to individuals and families suffering from unexpected life events, crisis situations, or difficult circumstances. Documentary requirements must be submitted.
4-Step Financial Assistance Process
1

Screening & Verification

For completion and correctness of documents

2

Assessment

For eligibility determination

3

Provision of Assistance

For validation and approval

4

Referral Services

Additional support connections

Roles of DMH Workers in Financial Assistance
  • Become well-acquainted with state laws and policies regarding financial assistance to individuals in crisis
  • Create a positive atmosphere during the situation
  • Be perceptive with the problem and the assistance clients are seeking
  • Be kind, sincere, understanding, and empathetic of the client's needs

Lesson 8 at a Glance Key Points Summary

📌 LESSON 8 — KEY TAKEAWAYS

Admin Procedures
Provide an objective set of rules governing DMH professionals and workers; ensure accountability for deviations.
Force Health Protection
Promotes worker resilience in all relief settings. Self-care is crucial to prevent compassion fatigue (Yin & Kukor, 2010).
3 DMH Phases
Pre-deployment · During deployment · Post-deployment — each with specific challenges and DMH supports.
Confidentiality
All MH practitioners are duty-bound to safeguard client info in obtaining, using, storing, and releasing it.
When to Disclose
Subpoenaed by court · Mandated/permitted by law · Client consent · Life-threatening emergency · Client is a minor · Child abuse experience.
Record Keeping
Essential for continuity of care, research, institutional compliance, billing, and legal requirements. Informed Consent = Client's Protection.
Informed Consent
Required before making written and oral reports about clients.
Financial Assistance
DSWD handles it. Procedures: (1) Screening & Verification → (2) Assessment → (3) Provision → (4) Referral Services.
DMH Worker Role
Be kind, empathetic, perceptive, and knowledgeable about relevant laws and policies for clients in crisis.
