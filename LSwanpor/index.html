Lesson 1: Introduction & Ethical Foundations

Defining Counseling vs. Psychotherapy, Contemporary Models, and Counselor Characteristics.

Defining Counseling vs. Psychotherapy

DimensionCounselingPsychotherapy
Treatment DurationRelatively brief & short-termLonger-term, in-depth treatment
Core FocusBehavior & specific present situations/symptomsThought processes, personality, chronic & past issues
Process TypeAction/Behavior-focused, secondary processFeeling/Experience-focused, primary process
GoalGuidance, problem-solving, immediate copingInsight, structural personality overhaul, talk therapy

Overview of Contemporary Counseling Models

Psychodynamic
Freud (Psychoanalytic) & Adler (Adlerian). Focuses on unconscious drives, early childhood determinants, and social interest/striving.
Experiential / Relationship
Existential (Frankl, May, Yalom), Person-Centered (Rogers), Gestalt (Perls). Focuses on human choice, awareness, and relationship quality.
Cognitive Behavioral (CBT)
Behavior (Skinner, Bandura), REBT (Ellis), Cognitive Therapy (Beck), Reality/Choice (Glasser). Focuses on action, learning, and thought restructuring.
Systems & Postmodern
Feminist, Solution-Focused Brief Therapy (de Shazer, Berg), Narrative (White, Epston), Family Systems (Bowen, Satir). Views reality as socially constructed.

Personal Characteristics of Effective Counselors

  • Authenticity & Identity: Counselors know who they are, respect themselves, and avoid hiding behind rigid professional facades or roles.
  • Cultural Competence: Respect for diversity, social class, gender, and personal value differences.
  • Humor & Present Awareness: Ability to laugh at mistakes, live in the "now," and acknowledge errors without dwelling on misery.
  • Healthy Boundaries: Ability to maintain balance, say "no," and avoid carrying client problems into personal leisure hours.

Care for the Counselor

Personal therapy serves as a critical training and self-care model. It builds self-awareness, empathy, tolerance, and prevents countertransference from harming clients. Research confirms clinicians with personal therapy build stronger therapeutic alliances.

Ethics, Values & Bracketing

Bracketing
Setting aside personal values to avoid value imposition and honor client worldviews (ACA Code Standard A.4.b).
Mandatory vs. Aspirational
Mandatory ethics fulfills minimum legal standards; aspirational ethics aims for the absolute best interest of the client (Positive Ethics).
Informed Consent
An ongoing, written/oral process outlining therapy goals, benefits, risks, fees, and confidentiality limits.
Confidentiality Exceptions
Client under 16 victim of rape/abuse, hospitalization required, legal court order, or client requests record release.

Lesson 2: Psychoanalytic Psychotherapy

Freudian Constructs, Personality Structure, Defense Mechanisms, and Psychosocial Growth.

Structure of Personality (Freud)

Id (Pleasure Principle)

Present at birth. Source of psychic energy and biological instincts. Illogical, impulsive, amoral, and completely unconscious "spoiled brat."

Ego (Reality Principle)

Executive branch that mediates between the Id's urges and external reality. Logical, planful, seat of intelligence and rationality.

Superego (Moral Code)

Judicial branch representing internalized societal/parental values. Strives for perfection rather than pleasure. Produces pride or guilt.

Types of Anxiety

  • Reality Anxiety: Fear of real danger from the external world, proportional to actual threat.
  • Neurotic Anxiety: Fear that instincts will get out of control and cause punishment (Ego vs. Id).
  • Moral Anxiety: Fear of one's own conscience and feelings of guilt (Ego vs. Superego).

Ego-Defense Mechanisms

MechanismDefinition & Clinical Behavior
RepressionInvoluntary removal of threatening/painful thoughts from conscious awareness. Basis of Freud's defenses.
DenialDistorting or "closing one's eyes" to trauma at preconscious/conscious levels.
Reaction FormationExpressing conscious attitudes completely opposite to disturbing unconscious desires (e.g., masking hate with fake love).
ProjectionAttributing one's own unacceptable impulses to others ("They are aggressive, not me").
DisplacementDirecting discharge of energy/hostility onto a safer, accessible target instead of the original threat.
RationalizationManufacturing "logical" good reasons to explain away disappointment or a bruised ego.
SublimationDiverting aggressive/sexual energy into socially acceptable, productive, or creative outlets.
RegressionReverting to earlier, immature developmental phases faced with severe stress (e.g., thumb-sucking).
Introjection / IdentificationSwallowing values of others / identifying with successful causes or aggressors to enhance self-worth.
CompensationMasking weaknesses by developing positive traits to balance limitations.

Psychosocial Stages (Erik Erikson)

Anna Freud expanded ego psychology, while Erikson introduced 8 lifespan psychosocial stages driven by developmental crises:

Trust vs. Mistrust→ Autonomy vs. Shame→ Initiative vs. Guilt→ Industry vs. Inferiority→ Identity vs. Role Conf.→ Intimacy vs. Isolation→ Generativity vs. Stagnation→ Integrity vs. Despair

Psychoanalytic Techniques

Free Association
Saying whatever comes to mind without censorship to access repressed unconscious material.
Interpretation
Explaining meanings of behaviors, dreams, and resistance when material is near conscious awareness.
Transference & Working-Through
Unconscious shifting of feelings onto therapist; working-through entails repetitive exploration of defenses.

Lesson 3: Jungian & Adlerian Approaches

Analytical Psychotherapy, Archetypes, Style of Life, and Reorientation Techniques.

Jungian Analytical Psychotherapy

Carl Jung emphasized Middle Age as the vital stage for growth. His core concepts include:

Archetypes & Collective Unconscious

Collective Unconscious: Universal inherited ancestral memories. Archetypes: Symbolic patterns including Shadow (dark side), Anima/Animus (opposite gender self), and Self (syzygy/wholeness).

Jungian Typology

2 Attitudes: Extraversion vs. Introversion.
4 Functions: Rational (Thinking/Feeling) and Irrational (Sensing/Intuition).

Adlerian Therapy & Style of Life

Alfred Adler focused on Gemeinschaftsgefühl (Social Interest), innate potential for community living shaped by the mother-child bond. Key constructs include:

  • Style of Life: Unique way of striving for perfection, set by ages 4-5 across 5 life tasks (Self, Spiritual, Work, Society, Love).
  • Inferiority vs. Superiority Complex: Universal inferiority feelings drive growth. Complex occurs when one feels incapable of solving problems; superiority complex masks inferiority with arrogance.
  • Basic Mistakes: Self-defeating beliefs (Overgeneralizations, False Security, Misperceptions, Minimized Worth, Faulty Values).

Adlerian Reorientation Techniques

TechniqueDescription & Clinical Purpose
ImmediacyAddressing nonverbal mismatches occurring in the present moment tentatively.
Acting As IfBehaving as though a desired behavior will succeed to build self-efficacy.
Catching OneselfNoticing unwanted behavior before/during execution ("Aha!" moment).
Spitting in the SoupExposing hidden purposes of unhelpful behavior to destroy its appeal.
Push-Button TechniqueSwitching between positive and negative memories to prove we control emotions.
Paradoxical IntentionPrescribing and exaggerating the symptom until it loses power.

Lesson 4: Existential Therapy

Philosophical Roots, Core Dimensions of Human Existence, and Therapeutic Presence.

Philosophical Foundations

Kierkegaard
Creative anxiety, despair, fear, dread, guilt, and confronting nothingness.
Nietzsche
Will to power, confronting mortality, death, and personal assertion.
Heidegger & Sartre
Authentic being, isolation, freedom, responsibility, choice, and meaninglessness.
Buber & Yalom
I-Thou genuine encounters, interpersonal connections, and ultimate concerns (death, freedom, isolation, meaninglessness).

Basic Dimensions of the Human Condition

  • Capacity for Self-Awareness: Freedom to choose expanded through awareness.
  • Freedom and Responsibility: We are the authors of our lives; avoiding choices leads to inauthenticity.
  • Identity & Striving for Meaning: Finding purpose in an inherently indifferent world.
  • Existential Anxiety & Death Awareness: Anxiety is an inevitable part of living; confronting mortality gives life urgency.

Existential Strategies & Interventions

Therapeutic Presence

The primary intervention is the authentic I-Thou relationship itself, grounded in deep listening and phenomenological attunement.

Telling the Story (Myth)

Clients share personal myths and narratives to integrate past, present, and future into a coherent, meaningful self.
