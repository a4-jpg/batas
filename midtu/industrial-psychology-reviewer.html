1.3 · Hiring

Staffing: matching people with jobs

The staffing process matches people with jobs through recruiting and selection. Recruiting is the process of generating a pool of qualified applicants for organizational jobs.

Key pointIf the number of available candidates only equals the number of people to be hired, no real selection is required: the choice has already been made. The organization must leave some openings unfilled or take every candidate.

What good recruiting requires

Recruiting is more than running a newspaper ad. To replace normal attrition and provide for growth, an employer needs to:

  • Know the business and industry to recruit qualified employees.
  • Identify keys to success in the labor market, including how to deal with competitors' recruiting.
  • Cultivate networks and relationships with sources of prospective employees.
  • Promote the company brand so it becomes known as a good place to work.
  • Create recruiting metrics to measure the effectiveness of recruiting efforts.

Administrative vs. strategic recruiting

Merely administrative

  • Coordinating internal openings
  • Handling the flow of candidate data
  • Regulatory reporting
  • Moving candidates through the system
  • Important, but not enough.

Strategic

  • Ties the employer's business strategy to its recruiting strategy.
  • Grows more important as labor markets shift and become competitive.
  • Strategic HR planning aligns HR strategies with organizational goals and plans.

Recruiting methods

Internal·External·Internet / web-based

Internal methods fill openings from within the organization, external methods reach outside candidates, and web-based methods use the internet to reach applicants.

Selection & testing principles

Why selection must be systematic, what behavior is measured, and what makes a good test.

1.4 · Preparing the testing program

Selection is everywhere

Selection is a process we are constantly involved in: in industry, clinic, school, and daily life. Sometimes we are selected (as friends, teammates, partners); sometimes we do the selecting. Usually selection rests on subjective factors such as how a person acts or looks, without systematic evaluation of qualifications and abilities.

Why it mattersIt is best to know the advantage of a selection process that is systematic and thorough.

Who uses psychometricians and testing

Psychometricians are looked upon as experts in selecting students entering school, job seekers recruited by firms, military personnel, those handling public office, and people providing all kinds of services. In the clinic they classify and categorize maladjustments before a psychotherapist is selected to handle patients.

Psychological testing as a selection technique

  • Tests determine specific abilities, attitudes, and characteristics, from ability to personality.
  • Testing may be complex and costly, but it is less complex and costly than selecting the wrong teacher, student, job holder, public official, or patient.

Which behavior do we test?

Physical and ability characteristics

  • General health and physical strength
  • Manual dexterity
  • Eye-hand coordination
  • Visual facility
  • Mechanical ability
  • Oral and written expression
  • Decision-making ability
  • Planning ability

Personality characteristics

  • Initiative
  • Emotional stability
  • Behavior under stress

Biographical inventories

Procedures that supplement tests by giving information on individual differences. They reflect habits and attitudes and cover:

Present home, spouse, childrenRecreation and hobbiesInterestsSchool and educationSelf-impressionValues, opinions, preferencesWork experience

Advantages of well-developed tests

Tests that are well-developed and soundly researched have advantages over other methods:

Standardization·Objectivity·Validity·Reliability·Norms

Group vs. individual vs. computer-assisted tests

Group tests

  • Administered to many people at the same time.
  • Useful when many must be tested at once.
  • Preferred for vocational guidance and counseling.

Individual tests

  • Preferred for children and for diagnostic work with emotionally disturbed persons.
  • Easier to establish rapport.
  • Possible to delve more deeply into the behavior measured.

Computer-assisted tests

  • Testing can occur any time a candidate applies, not only when a qualified administrator is available.
  • A wide range of abilities can be measured quickly.
  • Immediate feedback; scores in seconds.
  • Drawback: very expensive.

Four basic types of psychological tests

The most useful distinction is by the characteristic, trait, or behavior measured:

  1. Mental, psychomotor, and visual abilities
  2. Achievement
  3. Interests
  4. Personality
1.5 · Behavior to be measured

Basic human abilities

Individuals differ in their capacity to acquire specific skills and knowledge needed in certain jobs. These capacities are called aptitudes, which are really basic abilities. The term “human abilities” is elusive: lifting heavy loads, adding numbers, and remembering phone numbers are different abilities.

Intelligence tests

The range of abilities commonly called dexterity, manipulative ability, motor ability, precise eye-hand coordination, and muscular coordination: skilled muscular performance, often with visual control.

VI · Personality, interest & attitude

Why personality and interests matter at work

Personality and interests have a marked influence on work performance and on adjustment to family, health, society, emotion, and jobs. Personality factors bear directly on jobs with substantial personal contact: sales, public relations, supervision and management, interviewing.

1. Projective techniques

  • Not very common in business and industry; used for delicate, high positions such as executives; widely used in clinical practice.
  • An intentionally ambiguous stimulus (inkblot, picture); the examinee says what he sees.
  • Administrator must be skilled and well trained because he interprets the responses and patterns.
  • Examples: Rorschach Ink Blot, Thematic Apperception Test, Draw-a-Person (Goodenough or Machover), Sacks Sentence Completion, Bender-Gestalt.

2. Questionnaire inventories

  • Paper-and-pencil; no right or wrong answers, so “inventory” is more accurate than “test.”
  • Statements about feelings, fears, attitudes, values, beliefs. Example: “I feel at ease with strangers: yes / don't know / no.”
  • Scored on dimensions such as sociability, emotionality, impulsiveness, dominance.
Faking and the forced-choice techniqueBecause examinees may fake (give dishonest responses), constructors use forced-choice: sets of two or more statements where the examinee picks the one “most” or “least” like himself. These are also called self-report personality inventories.
