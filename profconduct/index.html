Only human acts can be praised or blamed. Ignorance, fear, violence, passion and habit lower or remove responsibility.

Applied ethics

Applies ethical theories to specific real-world problems and professions (medical, business, environmental, media, legal, police, public service). Other branches: metaethics (meaning of moral terms), normative (Kant, utilitarianism, virtue ethics), descriptive (what people actually believe).

Everyday scenarios

Lost cellphone
Do not keep, use or browse it. Turn it in to the teacher, school office or guard and ask for acknowledgement. Art. 308 RPC treats failure to deliver found property as theft.
Not a credit grabber
Name teammates and sources, never present others’ work as yours, do your fair share, share praise, own failures, correct wrong credit. Values: honesty, humility, fairness, integrity.

History & philosophy of ethics

Civilizations, Golden Rule, Socrates, karma, Kant, utilitarianism, virtue

Civilizations and the Golden Rule

Mesopotamia
Cradle of civilization (Tigris–Euphrates): cities, cuneiform, laws, the wheel. Code of Hammurabi (Babylon, about 1754 BC): retribution, “an eye for an eye”.
Ancient Greece
Cradle of Western civilization: philosophy, democracy, systematic ethics (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle).
Golden Rule
“Do unto others as you would have them do unto you”: ethic of reciprocity (Matthew 7:12, Confucius, many faiths). Negative form: the Silver Rule.

Thinkers and theories

WhoKey points
Socrates (470–399 BC)Wrote nothing; known via Plato. Socratic method of questioning. “The unexamined life is not worth living”; virtue is knowledge; wrongdoing comes from ignorance. Executed in 399 BC, drank hemlock.
Karma (India)Sanskrit “action”; cause and effect in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism; samsara (rebirth); related: dharma, moksha. People are responsible for intentions and actions.
Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)Deontological: rightness depends on duty and moral law, not consequences. Categorical Imperative: act on a rule you could will as universal law; treat humanity as an end, never merely a means. Lying is always wrong.
Bentham and MillUtilitarianism, consequentialist: greatest good for the greatest number. Bentham: quantity of pleasure; Mill: quality. Act vs. rule utilitarianism. Criticism: may sacrifice minorities and rights.
AristotleVirtue ethics (Nicomachean Ethics): good life (eudaimonia) from virtuous character; virtue is habit; Golden Mean (courage between cowardice and recklessness).
PlatoCardinal virtues: wisdom, courage, temperance, justice.
Exam tip: Kant = duty; Bentham and Mill = greatest good; Aristotle = virtue and the good life; Socrates = questioning; karma = India.

Search warrant

  • Art. III, Sec. 2: issued only on probable cause determined personally by the judge after examining the complainant and witnesses under oath; must particularly describe the place and things.
  • Rule 126: one specific offense; valid 10 days; served in daytime unless the affidavit justifies otherwise.
  • Witness rule (Sec. 8): lawful occupant or a family member present; if absent, two witnesses of sufficient age and discretion from the same locality.
  • No witnesses: search is irregular and illegal; evidence may be inadmissible (exclusionary rule); liability under Art. 130 RPC.
  • Probable cause: facts that lead a reasonably prudent person to believe an offense was committed and the items are in the place.

Custodial rights and confession

  • Art. III, Sec. 12: right to remain silent and to competent, independent counsel (provided if unaffordable). Waiver only in writing with counsel. No torture, force or secret or incommunicado detention. Violating confessions are inadmissible.
  • Extrajudicial confession: voluntary, with counsel, express, in writing; corroboration by corpus delicti needed (Rule 133).
  • Miranda comes from Miranda v. Arizona (US, 1966); implemented by Art. III Sec. 12 and RA 7438. Warning: silence; anything said can be used against you; right to a lawyer; one appointed if you cannot afford.
  • Custodial investigation (RA 7438): questioning by law enforcers after a person is in custody or significantly deprived of freedom; includes an “invitation”; begins when inquiry focuses on a particular suspect. Not covered: general on-scene questioning, spontaneous statements, statements to private persons, routine booking (check your module).

Bail and warrantless arrest

Bail (Art. III Sec. 13)
Denied for offenses punishable by reclusion perpetua or life imprisonment when evidence of guilt is strong; otherwise a right before conviction. Decided at a summary hearing; prosecution has the burden. Death penalty abolished by RA 9346 (2006).
Warrantless arrest (Rule 113 Sec. 5)
(a) In flagrante delicto; (b) hot pursuit with probable cause from personal knowledge; (c) escaped prisoner. Also a bailed accused who jumps bail. Deliver to the judicial authority within 12, 18 or 36 hours (Art. 125 RPC).

Public officers: laws & offenses

RA 6713, RA 3019, RPC offenses, administrative offenses

RA 6713 and RA 3019

LawKey points
RA 6713 (1989)Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards for Public Officials and Employees; covers all in government, including GOCCs, paid or not. SALN (Sec. 8): within 30 days of assuming office, by April 30 yearly, within 30 days after leaving. Violating Secs. 7, 8, 9: up to 5 years or up to ₱5,000, or both, plus possible disqualification. Prohibited acts (Sec. 7): financial interest in office transactions, conflicting outside work, misusing confidential information, soliciting or accepting gifts.
RA 3019 (1960)Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (Sec. 3): persuading another officer to violate rules; gifts or percentages from contracts; undue injury or unwarranted benefits through manifest partiality, evident bad faith or gross inexcusable negligence (e); neglecting to act to get a benefit (f); manifestly disadvantageous contracts (g); financial interest in a contract one intervenes in (h); approving permits for the unqualified (j); divulging secrets (k). Penalty: 6 years 1 month to 15 years, perpetual disqualification, confiscation of unexplained wealth.
Conflict of interest
Private interest conflicts or appears to conflict with public duty. Remedy: disclose, inhibit or recuse, divest.
Gifts
Manifestly excessive gifts are barred even at Christmas. Exception: unsolicited gifts of small or nominal value. Test: solicited? from someone dealing with the office? more than nominal? influences the act?

Revised Penal Code offenses

OffenseKey test
Public officer (Art. 203)Takes part in public functions by law, election or appointment, any rank; includes police
Qualified bribery (Art. 211-A)Law enforcer does not arrest or prosecute an offender (reclusion perpetua offense) in exchange for a gift; related: Art. 210 direct, 211 indirect, 212 corruption of officials (the giver)
Infidelity in custody of prisonersArt. 223: consents to or connives in escape. Art. 224: escape through negligence. Art. 225: custody by a non-public officer
Abuses against chastity (Art. 245)Indecent advances to a woman with a pending matter or in custody; arresto mayor and suspension
Abandonment (Art. 238)Leaving office before resignation is accepted, to the detriment of public service

Administrative offenses

Dishonesty
Concealing or distorting the truth about one’s office; lack of integrity (falsified PDS, cheating in civil service exam, false SALN). Grave offense: dismissal even on first offense.
Sexual harassment
RA 7877 (1995): authority, influence or moral ascendancy demanding a sexual favor. RA 11313 Safe Spaces Act (2019): streets, public spaces, online, workplaces. Forms: verbal, physical, visual, online, quid pro quo. CSC grades: grave, less grave, light.
Insubordination
Willful disregard of lawful, reasonable orders of a superior (order, knowledge, willful disobedience). Unlawful orders need not be obeyed.
Abandonment of duty
Leaving post without valid reason and with intent to relinquish; resignation takes effect only on acceptance. Absence without leave for 30 days or more: dropped from the rolls.
Loafing
Being away from post or wasting time in office hours without permission (e.g., returning at 2:30 p.m.). Check classification in your module.
Unanswered letter
Violation: respond within 15 working days (RA 6713 Sec. 5(a)). RA 11032: 3 working days simple, 7 complex, 20 highly technical.

Ranks (RA 11200)

GroupRanks, highest to lowest

Police Non-Commissioned OfficersExecutive Master Sergeant, Chief Master Sergeant, Senior Master Sergeant, Master Sergeant, Staff Sergeant, Corporal, Patrolman/Patrolwoman

Powers and functions (RA 6975 Sec. 24)

  • Enforce laws and ordinances protecting lives and property.
  • Maintain peace and order and ensure public safety.
  • Investigate and prevent crimes, arrest offenders, bring them to justice, assist prosecution.
  • Arrest, search and seize in accordance with the Constitution and law.
  • Detain an arrested person only for the legal period, informing him of his constitutional rights.
  • License firearms and explosives; supervise security agencies, guards and private detectives.
  • Perform other duties provided by law.
