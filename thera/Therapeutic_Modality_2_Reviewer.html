Dual origins of the TC

Two independent post-WWII traditions

Overview

The correction-based Therapeutic Community (TC) is a widely recognized treatment modality for drug-abusing offenders. It traces to two traditions.

FeatureBritish Democratic TCAmerican Concept-Based TC
FounderMaxwell Jones (1946/1954), “Father of Democratic TC”Charles “Chuck” Dederich (1958), Synanon
TargetPost-WWII British veterans with combat neurosis/PTSD; psychiatric patientsAlcoholics and substance abusers seeking a drug-free self-help movement
PhilosophyDemocratic group work, social learning, Socratic dialogue, “therapeutic use of groups”Hierarchy, behavior modification, self-help, forced personal change via confrontation
StructureFlatter hierarchy; staff and residents participate equallyStrict hierarchy; “older” residents are role models and supervisors
High-yieldTwo traditions: (1) British Democratic TC, Maxwell Jones, social learning; (2) American Concept-Based TC, Dederich/Synanon, behavior modification and structured self-help.

British Democratic TC

The Maxwell Jones model

Origin and five axioms

The term was coined by Thomas Main in 1946 (community therapy combined with psychoanalytic psychotherapy) and expanded by Maxwell Jones. It treats severe, long-standing disorders (“chronic failures”, “troublemakers”) through healthy group life.

  1. Two-way communication on all levels.
  2. Decision-making on all levels.
  3. Shared (multiple) leadership.
  4. Consensus in decision-making.
  5. Social learning by interaction in the “here and now”.
Socratic learningThe facilitator helps participants uncover knowledge from within the group instead of teaching new knowledge formally.

Four central principles

Permissiveness
Residents freely express thoughts and emotions without punishment or censure.
Democracy
Residents and staff have equal chances to participate in the organization.
Communalism
Face-to-face communication and free interaction create sharing and belonging.
Reality testing
Residents are continuously confronted with their own image and its impact as seen by peers and staff.
Key facility: Grendon Prison (UK, built 1959) has wings running TCs experimentally for psychologically disturbed offenders.

Legislation & research

Why prisons adopted TCs

The problem and the 1994 Crime Bill

Drug use among offenders
7 of 10 men (70%) and 8 of 10 women (80%) in the criminal justice system use drugs.
Revolving door
Without structured intervention, drug-involved offenders resume crime and drug use after release and return to custody.
RSAT
Residential Substance Abuse Treatment for State Prisoners Formula Grant Program, from the 1994 Crime Bill: $270 million over its first 5 years (from 1996).
Why
Research showed prison-based TCs significantly reduce recidivism and drug relapse. RSAT also encourages cognitive skills training, behavioral programming, vocational methods and 12-step programming.

TC in the Philippines

Timeline, agencies, key milestones

Timeline

WhenEvent
1970sIntroduced by the Drug Abuse Research Foundation, Inc. (DARE). First Asian TC: Bahay Pag-asa, Trece Martires, Cavite, for heroin addicts.
After 1971DARE expanded residential centers across Manila and Baguio City and helped export the TC model to Malaysia, Thailand and other Asian nations.
1997PPA secured funding from the U.S. State Department’s Bureau of International Narcotics and Law Enforcement Affairs (INL) for training by Daytop International.

Government integration

BJMP
Bureau of Jail Management and Penology: jails.
BuCor
Bureau of Corrections: prisons.
PPA
Parole and Probation Administration: community/outpatient supervision.
Treatment centers
NBI Treatment Center (Tagaytay) and the old Bicutan Treatment Center (formerly PNP, now under the DOH).

Participating agencies: DDB, DSWD, PNP, NBI, BuCor, BJMP, PPA, and local Anti-Drug Abuse Councils (Quezon City, Parañaque).
